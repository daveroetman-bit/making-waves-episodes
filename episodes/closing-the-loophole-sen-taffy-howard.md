## Summary

Sen. Taffy Howard of District 34, the prime sponsor of Amendment J, joins me to explain why South Dakota needs to say plainly in its Constitution that only U.S. citizens can vote. Right now our Constitution guarantees the vote to citizens, but it never says non-citizens can’t vote. J closes that loophole by adding non-citizens to the list of people disqualified from voting. This isn’t theoretical. About 20 local jurisdictions in three states plus D.C. already let non-citizens vote in local elections. New York City tried to open its municipal elections to roughly 800,000 non-citizens until the courts struck it down under the New York Constitution. And on Oct. 1, a federal judge in Florida threw out a non-citizen voting charge, ruling that the 1996 federal law couldn’t be applied in that case because voter qualifications are for the states to set. That’s exactly why this belongs in our state Constitution. We also cover Amendment K, Taffy’s unclaimed property trust fund, which passed the Legislature unanimously. Unclaimed property is your money, not tax money, and the state has already spent about $1.2 billion of it that it still owes back to rightful owners. K puts those dollars in a permanent trust, like our other state trust funds, so they earn more for South Dakota instead of being spent. And Taffy makes the case for Amendment I: if Washington ever drops its 90% share of Medicaid expansion, I ends the expansion coverage so South Dakota taxpayers aren’t stuck covering the difference out of the general fund. Yes on J, yes on K, yes on I.
