## Summary

Show notes Four neighbors from NOPE, Neighbors Opposed to Prison Expansion (Tom, Sam, Sarah and Madeline, introduced by first name only), join Dave Roetman to make the case against the state's plan to build a new men's penitentiary on 320 acres of farmland between Harrisburg and Canton in rural Lincoln County. They cover the history of the land, the neighbors' zoning lawsuit, the missing infrastructure and services at the site, road and water costs, and why they believe the state should rebuild on the existing Sioux Falls site. Recorded in August 2024, while the neighbors' zoning lawsuit was still pending. About the land: the 320 acres were the farm of Alfred and Ethilda "Tillie" Haug. The land passed to the state's Office of School and Public Lands in 1992 to support the common schools, and the Department of Corrections bought it from School and Public Lands for about $7.9 million in 2023. Update (Oct. 2026): The Lincoln County prison was never built. In August 2024 the Harrisburg City Council unanimously turned down the state's offer to connect the prison to the city's wastewater system, and in October 2024 a Lincoln County judge dismissed the zoning lawsuit brought by NOPE and neighboring landowners. In February 2025 the South Dakota House rejected funding for the $825 million Lincoln County prison (34-35, then a 35-35 tie on reconsideration). Gov. Larry Rhoden's Project Prison Reset task force then dropped the Lincoln County site. In July 2025 the South Dakota Supreme Court ruled against the neighbors, finding they had no right to enforce county zoning against the state, though by then the issue was largely moot. On Sept. 23, 2025, a special session of the Legislature approved a $650 million, roughly 1,500-bed prison on Benson Road in northeast Sioux Falls. As part of that land purchase, the 320 Lincoln County acres go back to private ownership under county zoning. Ground was broken in Sioux Falls on April 22, 2026, with completion expected around 2029-2030. (Sources are linked below.)
