Show notes

Published June 7, 2025, after Sen. Amber Hulse's first legislative session.

Guest

Sen. Amber Hulse (R), South Dakota Senate District 30 (Custer, Fall River and Pennington counties), in office since January 14, 2025. She grew up on a ranch south of Hot Springs, earned her law degree at Georgetown Law in 2024, and is vice chair of Senate Judiciary and a member of Senate Taxation (South Dakota Legislature, Ballotpedia, amberforsouthdakota.com). At the time of this interview she was an attorney with the Dhillon Law Group.

In the 2025 session she was the prime sponsor of SB 73, which requires anyone who registers to vote while applying for a South Dakota driver license to be a state resident for voting purposes. It passed the Senate 32-3 and the House 69-0 and was signed March 25, 2025 (South Dakota Legislature).

Background

Figures and accounts Hulse gives in this interview are her own: constituents seeing 70% property tax increases over five years (from $2,000-$3,000 to $8,000 a year); fewer than 50 veterans now served in the Hot Springs VA's mental health program; and, from election day 2024, threatening letters to voters and the invalid voter registrations she says were flagged in Pennsylvania.

Update (November 2026)

Property taxes: in March 2026, Gov. Larry Rhoden signed SB 96 (counties may adopt up to a half-cent sales tax that must go to owner-occupied property tax relief) and SB 245 (a homeowner property tax reduction fund), which supporters call the largest property tax cut in state history. Critics call it a tax shift onto sales tax. Hulse supported SB 96 and called it "a net decrease in your overall taxes," adding, "I hope we're still not done" (SDPB, KOTA, SD Department of Revenue).

Hot Springs VA: dialysis at the Hot Springs VA was paused in October 2025 over water-system problems. Hulse was the first to tell KOTA about the pause and asked who was making the decisions. In February 2026, Sen. Mike Rounds said the VA had committed $1 million to fix the water system and that VA Secretary Doug Collins assured him the facility won't close. Dialysis has since been reinstated, but the ICU and other services have not. At an Aug. 27, 2026 roundtable with Rounds, Hulse said veterans "shouldn't have to drive two hours for a 20-30 minute appointment" (KOTA, NewsCenter1).

Election: Hulse won the June 2, 2026 Republican primary for Senate District 30 over former Sen. Julie Frye-Mueller, 66.5% to 33.5%, and is unopposed in the Nov. 3, 2026 general election. She says property taxes are still the number-one issue she hears about (Ballotpedia, SDPB).
