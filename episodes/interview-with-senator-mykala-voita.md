## Summary

Recorded in January 2026, in the first weeks of South Dakota's 2026 legislative session. Guest Sen. Mykala Voita (R-Bonesteel), District 21; member of the Joint Committee on Appropriations Update (October 2026) SB 124, Voita and Rep. John Sjaarda's lab-grown meat bill, passed the Senate 29-5 and the House 63-4, and Gov. Larry Rhoden signed it on March 12. As amended, it bans the sale, manufacture and distribution of cell-cultured protein in South Dakota for five years, from July 1, 2026, to June 30, 2031, shorter than the 10-year sunset described in the interview. Rep. Julie Auch's separate bill, HB 1077, which would have listed lab-grown meat as adulterated food, passed both chambers but was vetoed by the governor on Feb. 10; the House fell short of an override, 32-32, on March 30. SCR 604 was amended in the Senate to urge South Dakotans to "seek" the Lord Most High for His healing presence and mercy, and was adopted 22-11 in the Senate on Jan. 27 and 42-23 in the House on Jan. 29. HB 1064, Rep. John Shubeck's direct-to-consumer meat trigger bill, passed the House 48-18 and the Senate 28-6 and was signed on Feb. 17. It takes effect only if federal law changes. SB 88, on surveying private land for possible condemnation, passed 34-0 and 66-0 and was signed on March 12. The eminent domain constitutional amendment, HJR 5001, passed the House 62-5 but failed in the Senate 14-19 on March 2 (Voita voted yes), so it is not on the November ballot. The FY2027 budget, adopted March 12, gave health care providers who serve Medicaid patients, including community support providers, a 1.4% increase, up from the governor's flat December proposal. HB 1251, a bill Voita co-sponsored to create a fund to help bring provider rates up to their full methodology, was deferred to the 41st day in House Appropriations. Voita won the June 2 Republican primary over former Sen. Erin Tobin, 59% to 41%, and faces Democrat Dan Andersson on the Nov. 3 ballot. Links Sen.
