Closing the Loophole: Sen. Taffy Howard Explains Amendment J (and Why She's Yes on K and I)

Show notes

Sen. Taffy Howard of District 34, the prime sponsor of Amendment J, joins me to explain why South Dakota needs to say plainly in its Constitution that only U.S. citizens can vote. Right now our Constitution guarantees the vote to citizens, but it never says non-citizens can’t vote. J closes that loophole by adding non-citizens to the list of people disqualified from voting.

This isn’t theoretical. About 20 local jurisdictions in three states plus D.C. already let non-citizens vote in local elections. New York City tried to open its municipal elections to roughly 800,000 non-citizens until the courts struck it down under the New York Constitution. And on Oct. 1, a federal judge in Florida threw out a non-citizen voting charge, ruling that the 1996 federal law couldn’t be applied in that case because voter qualifications are for the states to set. That’s exactly why this belongs in our state Constitution.

We also cover Amendment K, Taffy’s unclaimed property trust fund, which passed the Legislature unanimously. Unclaimed property is your money, not tax money, and the state has already spent about $1.2 billion of it that it still owes back to rightful owners. K puts those dollars in a permanent trust, like our other state trust funds, so they earn more for South Dakota instead of being spent. And Taffy makes the case for Amendment I: if Washington ever drops its 90% share of Medicaid expansion, I ends the expansion coverage so South Dakota taxpayers aren’t stuck covering the difference out of the general fund.

Yes on J, yes on K, yes on I.

Amendment J: the Attorney General’s explanation

Here is the Attorney General’s official ballot explanation for Amendment J, word for word as filed with the Secretary of State:

Title: An Amendment to the South Dakota Constitution Clarifying that an Individual Must Be a Citizen of the United States to be Eligible to Vote.

Explanation:

Article VII, § 2 of the South Dakota Constitution defines who is qualified to vote in state elections. Among these qualifications are age, residency, and United States citizenship requirements. Article VII, § 2 also establishes that individuals may be disqualified from voting because of mental incompetence or a felony conviction.

This constitutional amendment adds people who are not United States citizens to the list of individuals disqualified from voting, clarifying the existing requirement that only citizens may vote in state elections.

“Vote ‘Yes’ to adopt the amendment.” “Vote ‘No’ to leave the Constitution as it is.”

Source: Final Attorney General’s Statement for SJR 503 (Amendment J), filed with the SD Secretary of State on May 14, 2026 (PDF)
