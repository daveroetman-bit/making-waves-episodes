Why South Dakota Needs an Unclaimed Property Trust Fund

Show notes

Published Feb. 26, 2025, during the legislative session, after the South Dakota Senate passed both SB 155 and SJR 505 by 35-0 votes on Feb. 20, 2025, and while both were waiting for House hearings.

Guests

Josh Haeder, South Dakota State Treasurer

Jason Williams, Deputy State Treasurer

The bills

SB 155 (2025): caps how much unclaimed property money goes into the general fund each year and puts everything above the cap into a new trust fund for unclaimed property. In the conversation Haeder describes the cap as starting at $65 million; in the final law it is the office's operating costs plus $61,384,827 in fiscal 2026 and $58 million in fiscal 2027, then $4 million less each year down to $25 million from fiscal 2035 on.

SJR 505 (2025): asks voters to add a trust for unclaimed property fund to the state constitution, invested by the State Investment Council. Part of the interest and income goes to the general fund each year starting July 1, 2027, and the money can't be diverted except to pay owners' claims or by a three-fourths vote of all the members elected to each house.

Update (October 2026)

Both measures passed without a single no vote. The House passed SB 155 70-0 on March 5, 2025, and Gov. Larry Rhoden signed it March 13, 2025. The House passed SJR 505 69-0 on March 6, 2025; as a joint resolution it doesn't go to the governor and was filed with the Secretary of State on March 12, 2025.

SJR 505 is now Constitutional Amendment K on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot. Haeder wrote the "yes" statement in the state's ballot question pamphlet: the temporary fund earns only 3% to 4%, while the Investment Council's trusts do better (School and Public Lands 8.3%, Education Enhancement 7.9%). No "no" statement was filed, and South Dakota Searchlight reported on Oct. 15, 2026 that no organized opposition has surfaced. Searchlight also reported that yearly unclaimed property receipts recently hit a record $308 million.

The 2026 Legislature has already tapped the statutory fund once: SB 121, signed March 30, 2026, moved $8,212,497 from the trust fund for unclaimed property to the general fund for the Richmond Lake dam and spillway replacement.

Haeder is term-limited. In the episode he calls Williams "your next state treasurer," but Williams is not on the 2026 ballot; the Nov. 3 race for treasurer is between Republican Melissa Hull, who works in Haeder's office, and Democrat Margaret Kuipers. Early voting is underway, and Election Day is Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026.
