## Summary

Show notes Published Aug. 19, 2025. Recorded in August 2025, as Don Haggar was launching his campaign for the South Dakota Public Utilities Commission. Guest Don Haggar, Republican of Sioux Falls, served in the South Dakota House for District 10 from 2013 until he resigned on June 27, 2017, to become state director of Americans for Prosperity-South Dakota. He was majority whip in 2015-2016 and speaker pro tempore in 2017 (SD Legislature; SDPB, June 27, 2017). He has a background in real estate, banking and insurance, and an MBA from the University of South Dakota (South Dakota Searchlight; KOTA, Oct. 22, 2026). Background The Public Utilities Commission has three elected members serving staggered six-year terms. It regulates electric, natural gas and telephone utilities, rules on permits for power plants, pipelines and transmission lines, and licenses and inspects grain warehouses and grain buyers (SD PUC; Ballotpedia). The seat: Commissioner Gary Hanson, a Republican and former Sioux Falls mayor, was first elected to the PUC in 2002 and re-elected in 2008, 2014 and 2020. He did not run again in 2026 (KELOLAND; Ballotpedia). Texas, 2021: during Winter Storm Uri in February 2021, the extreme cold that knocked out power in Texas also strained the Southwest Power Pool, which called rolling blackouts that cut power for thousands of South Dakotans on Feb. 16, 2021 (SDPB, Feb. 17, 2021). Spain, 2025: on April 28, 2025, Spain's grid lost about 15 gigawatts, roughly 60% of the country's demand, within seconds, and Spain and Portugal went dark (POLITICO, April 28, 2025). The European grid operators' final report (ENTSO-E, March 20, 2026) found no single cause: voltage swings, gaps in voltage and reactive-power control and a cascade of generators tripping offline. Haggar's points about nuclear being taken offline and France's help are his own account. Dave's story of the backup coal plant in his hometown of Rock Rapids, Iowa, being sold and shipped to China is his own account and was not checked for these notes. Update (November 2026) June 27, 2026: Haggar won the Republican nomination for the PUC at the state convention in Rapid City with about 55% of the delegate vote, beating Rep. Peri Pourier of Pine Ridge (South Dakota Searchlight; SDPB). His Democratic opponent is Frank Kloucek of Scotland, a farmer and former state senator and representative. 2026 session: the Legislature passed, and Gov. Larry Rhoden signed, two data center laws. One lets the PUC bill data center companies for the cost of regulatory reviews. The other requires water providers to check whether a large data center's water use fits the local supply, requires electric providers to set separate terms for large data centers and recover the costs they cause, and keeps local governments' power to regulate them. Lawmakers rejected both a data center sales tax break and a moratorium (South Dakota Searchlight, Sept. 1, 2026). September 2026: Kloucek called for a one-year moratorium on new AI data centers. Haggar called the idea "reckless and pandering" and said the new laws are "the right foundation for how these decisions should be made going forward, case by case, on their unique merits" (South Dakota Searchlight, Sept. 1, 2026). In late October the two also split over the proposed NorthWestern Energy and Black Hills Corp. merger; Kloucek, who spoke against it, noted the PUC approved it 3-0. Haggar said he is "all for it" if mergers cut costs and deliver power more efficiently, and he has campaigned on affordability, reliability and protecting consumers (Dakota News Now, Oct. 27, 2026). Haggar has also said the job is quasi-judicial, so a commissioner "can't take strong positions for or against any sort of development" (KOTA, Oct. 22, 2026), the same point he makes in this interview.
