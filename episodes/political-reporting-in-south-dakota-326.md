## Summary

Austin Goss, then the Pierre-based reporter for The Dakota Scout, joins Dave Roetman to talk about the state of political reporting in South Dakota. He explains why Pierre's distance from Sioux Falls and Rapid City, a part-time citizen legislature and one-party rule make for a quiet capitol with few reporters, and why South Dakotans tend to show up only when a bill steps on their toes, like the 2022 Custer State Park campground expansion. Dave says the price of freedom is eternal vigilance and conservatives need to keep showing up. Goss also explains why The Dakota Scout keeps all of its reporting and printing in South Dakota, and why he believes independent, locally owned outlets are the future of political news. Recorded in August 2023. Update (Oct. 2026): The Dakota Scout is still publishing statewide news, and South Dakota Searchlight is still covering the Capitol. Goss kept reporting from Pierre for the Scout through the 2025 legislative session, including the Legislature's vote to ban eminent domain for carbon pipelines. In an Aug. 31, 2025, column he announced that he and his family were leaving South Dakota and heading back east, after he'd spent most of that year on active-duty military orders.
