## Summary

Show notes Amanda Radke is a rancher from Mitchell, a mother of four, and an agricultural writer and speaker who has covered farm policy for close to 20 years. She and her husband, Tyler, raise cattle. In this conversation she explains why she opposed Referred Law 21, the Senate Bill 201 "Landowner Bill of Rights" passed by the 2024 Legislature in response to Summit Carbon Solutions' proposed carbon dioxide pipeline, and why she believed it took away local control while doing nothing about eminent domain. Recorded in October 2024 and first published Oct. 25, 2024, ahead of that year's general election. Update (Oct. 2026): South Dakota voters rejected Referred Law 21 on Nov. 5, 2024, 59.4% to 40.6% (242,459 votes to 165,682, certified), repealing SB 201. In March 2025 the Legislature passed HB 1052, and Gov. Larry Rhoden signed it on March 6, 2025, banning the use of eminent domain for carbon dioxide pipelines. Days later Summit asked the PUC to suspend its South Dakota permit application indefinitely. In 2026 lawmakers passed SB 88, the "Bossly bill" limiting pipeline surveys without a landowner's permission (signed March 12, 2026), but the Senate killed HJR 5001, a proposed constitutional amendment on eminent domain for private gain, 19-14 on March 2, 2026. In May 2026 Summit described a new route west through Nebraska to Wyoming that does not include South Dakota, while saying its South Dakota easements and partners remain "valuable optionality for future phases." Amanda is still leading the property-rights fight and helped organize the January 2026 property rights rally at the Capitol in Pierre. (Sources are linked below.)
