Show notes

Published March 8, 2025, near the end of the legislative session. HB 1026 (Richmond Lake dam) had passed both chambers and was waiting for the governor, and HB 1266 (Brand Board) had just died in the Senate Agriculture and Natural Resources committee on March 6.

Guest

Brock Greenfield, South Dakota Commissioner of School and Public Lands (in office since January 2023; former state senator and representative, 22 years in the Legislature)

The bills

HB 1026 (2025): an appropriation to replace the Richmond Lake dam and spillway, about five miles northwest of Aberdeen, and for general maintenance and repair of other state-owned dams. In the conversation Greenfield puts the total price tag in the mid-$20 million range, with this bill providing the remaining roughly $13 million.

HB 1266 (2025): would have attached the South Dakota State Brand Board to the Office of School and Public Lands for reporting purposes, instead of the Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources. Greenfield's office was neutral.

Update (October 2026)

HB 1026 passed the House 70-0 on Feb. 24, 2025 and the Senate 32-2 on Feb. 27, and Gov. Larry Rhoden signed it March 13, 2025. The Legislature's Joint Committee on Appropriations lists it as $13.05 million in general funds, on top of $6.5 million in 2022 (SB 52) and $3.15 million in general funds plus federal spending authority in 2024 (SB 70), of which the office received about $2.94 million in federal money.

The mid-$20 million estimate didn't hold. After bids were opened on March 27, 2026, the project's estimated cost rose to $34,891,893. The 2026 Legislature passed SB 121, signed March 30, 2026, which moved $8,212,497 from the state's trust fund for unclaimed property to the general fund and appropriated it to Greenfield's office for the dam. The Joint Committee on Appropriations now asks the commissioner to report on construction progress and spending at its July and December meetings until the money is spent.

Construction is underway. The contractor's Oct. 23, 2026 update says the seepage cutoff wall is finished, the old spillway concrete has been removed, a test section of the new stepped spillway has been poured and the lake is being held at 1,349 feet. KELOLAND News reported in July 2026 that the project is expected to be finished in June 2028.

HB 1266 passed the House 47-22 on Feb. 21, 2025. In the Senate Agriculture and Natural Resources committee, a motion to pass it failed 3-4 and it was deferred to the 41st day 4-3 on March 6, 2025, which killed it. The 2026 Legislature took up other Brand Board bills, including HB 1020, which raised the maximum livestock ownership inspection fee and was signed Feb. 23, 2026, and HB 1267, which would have required statewide ownership inspection and was deferred to the 41st day in House committee. Neither moved the Brand Board to School and Public Lands.

The office's facts page now lists a $275 million Permanent Trust Fund and about $19.59 million a year apportioned to school districts and endowed institutions, along with 98 state-owned dams and 5.5 million mineral acres. (In the episode Greenfield cites the fund at more than $430 million; the two figures may be measured differently.)

Greenfield is running for a second term. He won the Republican nomination at the state party convention on June 27, 2026, over James Bialota, and faces Democrat Raeann Mettler on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026. His current term ends in January 2027.

I wasn't thrilled when I found out that, well, they had the 30% design presentation five months ago or so, but yesterday was the 60% design presentation, and they're looking at maybe elevated costs in certain areas. But they've already stipulated that it's their intention to try to find ways to address some of those and seek some cost savings. Because I said, "Look, the legislature has been very clear. They want this to get done. They want it to get done right, but they want it to get done within budget. And if I have to go back with my hat in hand next year and say, 'Yeah, we fell a little short, we need a couple million more,' that isn't going to go over well." So we've given them their marching orders to continue to look at turning loose a structurally sound product, but to do it within budget. They understood our message loud and clear. But yeah, it's a vitally important project to so many, not just in Brown County and Aberdeen, but all the way to, call it, the east-central part of the state.

Dave Roetman 35:09

Well, as an engineer will tell you, dams are temporary. All dams are temporary, and they eventually will have to be replaced. So that's an important project. Moving on, there's other legislation, the Brand Board legislation. Now, I know what the Brand Board is, but could you explain what the Brand Board is to people and why it's relevant to your office?

Brock Greenfield 35:09

Well, it didn't get rolled into our office in any way, but there was a bill. And just let me go back. The Brand Board has existed in South Dakota for nearly a century, somewhere thereabouts, and it was operated by the Stockgrowers up until 2008. Dave, I didn't mention, but I was a legislator for 22 years, and so I've fought a lot of fights and heard a lot of fights, and there were a lot of times issues arose relative to the Brand Board. So I know that this issue has persisted for a long time. In 2008, the responsibility was taken away from the Stockgrowers, if you will, and it was transferred over to the Attorney General's office, in DCI. And more recently it was put under the Department of Agriculture, which has now become the Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources, with the merger of the Department of Environment and Natural Resources and the Department of Ag. So it's just been a hot-button issue for a long time. Some of the brand owners across the state have been really seeking answers to a lot of questions they've had, and they don't feel satisfied. I don't want to besmirch anybody; I'm just trying to lay out there what I heard in testimony. They feel unsatisfied that they're getting the answers that they've sought, and they wanted to place the Brand Board under the Office of School and Public Lands only as it related to reporting, for reporting purposes. They felt that this office is an elected office and would be responsive to the people, and they didn't feel that maybe in its current structure that that's happening. And so they wanted to take it out of the realm of the bureaucracy, if you will, and place it under an elected office.

Brock Greenfield 40:02

I talked with my team, and I said, "I think we can manage this if the legislature sees fit to attach it to us for that purpose." The Brand Board is still autonomous, insofar as it's an informational budget. They are able to do their day-to-day operations without the heavy hand of government, I guess, if you will. Whether it's under the Attorney General's office or the Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources, or whether it was in some way transferred over to this office, they would still be autonomous, but they would have to report to us, and we would have to make sure that checks are getting cashed in a timely fashion, that there are no audit findings, and so on and so forth. I said if this happens, one of the things that I'll do immediately is I'll call for a special audit, basically a broad, sweeping audit, to hit reset. I don't want to take on responsibility for something that hasn't been given an all-clear. And I'm not alleging anything. I'm just saying I think it would be a good management practice for this office to make sure that we are starting with a clean slate. And maybe that audit gives them the all-clear, and we move forward, go ahead, everything's hunky-dory. Or maybe they would come up with some audit findings that need to be addressed. So in the end, it passed the House overwhelmingly. It came out of House committee with a strong vote, and it passed the House floor with a very strong vote. Yesterday, it hit a snag. It was killed in the Senate committee. I appeared only as a neutral party. I was asked a question or a couple questions, but I was neutral. I didn't testify as a proponent. I didn't testify as an opponent. I assured them that I trust the legislature's judgment, and if the legislature sees fit to place this under our office, I will accommodate. I said, "I know the history. I know that this has often been a hot-button issue. And if the legislature would choose to place this under my office and the office of future commissioners going forward, I believe that we would have the human resources to make the accommodations, to comply with all the reporting requirements, and to make sure that the reports are being filed in a timely fashion." And, as I said, that the checks are being cashed by those folks when they come in. That's been one of the issues. I don't want to get too into the weeds, and I don't want to speak ill of anybody, but I know that that was one of the audit findings just a couple years ago. And I've heard more recently that people have been sending their checks in for renewal, and those checks haven't been cashed, and they've been in their office since October, November and December. That's one of the things that I've learned in this office: when we get a check, it has to be processed within the next day. We get it up to the Treasurer's Office. We don't sit on it. That's a big deal to people. And I think that's one of the reasons that there remain some questions. But the Senate Ag Committee killed that bill. It was one that we were watching, one that we certainly had a keen interest in, and we wanted to know if our day-to-day operations were going to be changed because of the passage of a bill. We also recognized that it still had a couple obstacles to go through in the legislative process. And then the governor: if the bill would have passed, or if it gets resurrected and does get passed, that doesn't mean that it's a law yet. The governor still has the opportunity to weigh in on that. And I'm certainly respectful of the governor, and I'm certainly respectful of the Department of Ag, I should say Agriculture and Natural Resources, and the tough situation that they have. It's been a very tough issue, no matter who's been in charge of it, for a lot of years, and it probably will continue to be a hot-button issue. Now, I want to make note that they're reconstituting their board. Yesterday there were three confirmation hearings in committee. One of those people is the former commissioner of the Office of School and Public Lands. Jarrod Johnson received a unanimous vote in committee; he'll go to the floor next Monday. A couple others will go to the floor, one with an "advise and consent" recommendation, one with a "do not advise and consent," but the Senate will decide whether or not to confirm them.

Brock Greenfield 45:08

But the Brand Board then will presumably have four or five members after next Monday, and they'll be in a much better situation going forward. They've had two people confirmed earlier this year, but they were kind of in a weird state where some appointments hadn't been made. There were people that remained on there, without being reappointed, to conduct their ongoing business, but they hadn't been confirmed or even reappointed. So anyway, that's a long answer to a question. We were neutral on the bill, but we would have been happy to try to help remedy any situations that have been outstanding for a number of years. I don't envy anybody who has to work in that realm. I feel for the many interested parties who came forward and testified, either as proponents or opponents. There were ag producers on both sides of the issue. There were different groups, the associations, that testified on either side. And so what I want to see is for the folks who are at the helm to help get this thing fixed. And I do think that with Jarrod Johnson, who presumably will be a new member, I think he's got a breadth of knowledge. He's a brand owner. In fact, he says he owns two brands in South Dakota, one in Oklahoma, and I think he said one in, well, one of the A's, Arkansas or Alabama or somewhere. He certainly has a keen interest. He's a farmer and rancher. He's from the eastern side of the state, but also has a ranch operation in Meade County. So he knows the issues well, and so do the rest of those folks who are on there. So I do trust them. From my perspective, by the way, again, I didn't have a vested interest in this. I would have done what the legislature told me to do. But I said, if the bill dies, life on our end doesn't change. And if it does pass and get signed into law, we just have a little bit more responsibility, but we will make sure that it works. Our days in the office aren't confined to eight to five. We do the job we've got to do to get the job done, and there are a lot of nights when I leave here after seven o'clock, and some of those nights other people are still in the office. So we've got a very dedicated staff, and we just want to see that whatever it is that we're charged with is running as smoothly as possible here for the people of South Dakota.

Wow. I think you've established that the Office of School and Public Lands is kind of a big deal. And you have a final thought?
