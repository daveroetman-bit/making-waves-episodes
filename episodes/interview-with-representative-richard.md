## Summary

Show notes Published May 11, 2025, after the 2025 legislative session. Rep. Richard Vasgaard (R-Centerville) was in his first year back in the House after serving District 17 in 2021-2022. Guest Richard Vasgaard, South Dakota state representative for District 16 (Lincoln, Turner and Union counties) in 2025 and for District 17 in 2021-2022; a Turner County farmer with a bachelor's degree in agronomy from South Dakota State University Background HB 1085 (2025), Vasgaard's CO2 pipeline safety bill, would have put a moratorium on building carbon dioxide pipelines in South Dakota until new federal safety rules were in place. The House passed it 40-30 on Feb. 25, 2025. On March 4, 2025, the Senate State Affairs Committee voted 5-4 to defer it to the 41st legislative day, which killed it. HCR 6013 (2025), Vasgaard's resolution, supports the Electoral College, denounces the National Popular Vote Interstate Compact and invites other states to form an Electoral College Interstate Compact. The House adopted it 70-0 on Feb. 25, 2025, and the Senate concurred 34-0 on March 6, 2025. Also in 2025, the Legislature passed HB 1052, which bans the use of eminent domain for pipelines that carry carbon dioxide. Gov. Larry Rhoden signed it on March 6, 2025. Update (October 2026) Richard Vasgaard: Rep. Vasgaard died following a farming accident on his farm near Centerville on Aug. 27, 2025. He was 75. Gov. Larry Rhoden ordered flags flown at half-staff statewide on Sept. 2, 2025, the day of his funeral at Scandia Lutheran Church in Centerville, and called him "an honorable, God-fearing man who served our state with unwavering commitment." District 16: On Sept. 17, 2025, Gov. Rhoden appointed John Shubeck of Beresford, a Marine Corps veteran and farmer, to finish Vasgaard's term. In the June 2, 2026, Republican primary for District 16's two House seats, Lisa Bogue (2,192 votes) and Shubeck (1,947) advanced over Jason VanDenTop (1,879). The general election is Nov. 3, 2026. The CO2 pipeline: On May 13, 2026, Summit Carbon Solutions described a new route running west through Nebraska to Wyoming that does not include South Dakota. The company said its South Dakota partners and easements remain options for future phases.
