## Summary

Show notes Sarah Ulmer, who lives southeast of Harrisburg and is a member (not a board member) of Neighbors Opposing Prison Expansion (NOPE), joins Dave Roetman to talk about the state's plan to build a new men's penitentiary on farmland between Harrisburg and Canton in rural Lincoln County. She explains how neighbors learned about it from the news, with no signage, letters or public hearing, how the land came to the state, and why she believes a prison doesn't belong in a growing community of farm families and acreages. Recorded in fall 2023, a few weeks after the Department of Corrections announced the Lincoln County site on Oct. 6, 2023. About the land: the 320 acres were the farm of Alfred and Ethilda "Tillie" Haug. Tillie Haug died in 1990 without a will or heirs, and the land passed to the state's Office of School and Public Lands in 1992 to support the common schools. The Department of Corrections bought it from School and Public Lands for about $7.9 million in 2023. Update (Oct. 2026): The Lincoln County prison was never built. In August 2024 the Harrisburg City Council unanimously turned down the state's offer to connect the prison to the city's wastewater system, and in October 2024 a Lincoln County judge dismissed the zoning lawsuit brought by NOPE and neighboring landowners. In February 2025 the South Dakota House rejected funding for the $825 million Lincoln County prison (34-35, then a 35-35 tie on reconsideration). Gov. Larry Rhoden's Project Prison Reset task force then dropped the Lincoln County site. In July 2025 the South Dakota Supreme Court ruled against the neighbors, finding they had no right to enforce county zoning against the state, though by then the issue was largely moot. On Sept. 23, 2025, a special session of the Legislature approved a $650 million, roughly 1,500-bed prison on Benson Road in northeast Sioux Falls. As part of that land purchase, the 320 Lincoln County acres go back to private ownership under county zoning. Ground was broken in Sioux Falls on April 22, 2026, with completion expected around 2029-2030. (Sources are linked below.)
