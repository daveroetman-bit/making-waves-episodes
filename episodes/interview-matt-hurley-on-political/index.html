Show notes

Matt Hurley, a senior strategist at Southeastern Strategies, joins Dave Roetman in September 2025 to discuss political campaigns, marketing and the 2026 South Dakota governor’s race. Hurley describes growing up in southwestern Florida and coming into politics through marketing and social media, and says his firm works mostly on Republican primaries in swing states such as Florida, North Carolina, Michigan and Arizona. He recounts meeting President Trump at Mar-a-Lago in December 2023 and explains why he stayed with Trump. Hurley’s firm represents Toby Doeden, then a candidate for governor, and he contrasts Doeden with Governor Larry Rhoden, Congressman Johnson and Speaker Hansen, describing Doeden’s bus tour of more than 100 stops. He argues that voters care most about kitchen-table issues such as property taxes, education and public safety, and says the political divide is smaller than the media suggests.

Topics: political consulting, campaign marketing, social media, Donald Trump, 2026 governor’s race, Toby Doeden campaign, bus tour, retail politics, property taxes, populism, political divide

“You’re not just going to come into the state and buy TV ads and waltz your way into office.” - Matt Hurley

Show notes

Matt Hurley is a senior strategist at Southeastern Strategies, a political consulting firm that focuses mostly on Republican primaries. He joined Dave Roetman in September 2025, while his firm was working for Toby Doeden’s campaign for the Republican nomination for South Dakota governor. Recorded September 2025 and first published Sept. 16, 2025.

Update (Oct. 2026): Doeden finished first in the June 2, 2026 Republican primary with about 31% of the vote, ahead of Gov. Larry Rhoden (25%), Rep. Dusty Johnson (23%) and House Speaker Jon Hansen (21%). No one reached the 35% needed to win outright, so Doeden and Rhoden met in the first statewide primary runoff in South Dakota history on July 28. Rhoden won it 70.8% to 29.2% (83,590 votes to 34,435 in the official canvass). Doeden conceded that night, said he would support Rhoden, and thanked his campaign team. Rhoden faces Democrat Dan Ahlers in the Nov. 3 general election. (Sources are linked below.)
