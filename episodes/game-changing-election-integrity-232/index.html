Show notes

Published Feb. 18, 2025, during the legislative session and shortly after the South Dakota House passed both of Rep. Tony Kayser's election integrity bills: HB 1208 (46-22 on Feb. 12, 2025) and HB 1066 (47-22 on Feb. 13, 2025).

Guest

Rep. Tony Kayser (R-Sioux Falls), South Dakota House, District 14 (2025-2026); prime sponsor of HB 1066 and HB 1208

The bills

HB 1066 (2025): revise residency requirements for the purposes of voter registration. A resident is someone who keeps a fixed dwelling in South Dakota where they live and usually sleep for at least 30 consecutive days.

HB 1208 (2025): an applicant who gives only a mail-forwarding service or post office box address, with no description of where they actually live, may register or vote absentee only as a federal voter (president, U.S. Senate and U.S. House).

In the conversation the two bill numbers get swapped at times. In the final laws, HB 1066 is the 30-consecutive-day residency bill and HB 1208 is the federal-only ballot bill (HB 1208 also carries the same residency definition).

Update (October 2026)

Both bills became law. The Senate passed HB 1066 23-11 and HB 1208 25-9 on March 10, 2025, the House agreed to the Senate amendments on March 11, and Gov. Larry Rhoden signed both on March 25, 2025. The federal-only ballot for mail-forwarding and P.O. box voters took effect Jan. 1, 2026.

The Legislature built on the federal-only category in 2026. SB 175, the "South Dakota SAVE Act," signed March 26, 2026 with an emergency clause, requires new registrants to prove U.S. citizenship; anyone who doesn't is also registered as a federal-only voter.

The numbers are growing. South Dakota Searchlight counted more than 1,500 federal-only voters ahead of the June 2, 2026 primary, and 5,735 on the Secretary of State's list as of Oct. 6, 2026. More than 4,800 of them use a mail-forwarding or P.O. box address. Some snowbirds and other voters told Searchlight they didn't know they had been switched to federal-only status.

The League of Women Voters of South Dakota sued Secretary of State Monae Johnson in August 2026 over the notices sent to voters under SB 175, not over Kayser's residency laws. On Sept. 18, 2026, after the state sent corrected notices and new guidance to county auditors, the federal court denied a preliminary injunction.

Kayser finished third in the June 2, 2026 Republican primary for the two District 14 House seats (Taylor Rehfeldt 2,457, Tyler Tordsen 1,678, Kayser 1,627), so he is not on the Nov. 3 ballot.
