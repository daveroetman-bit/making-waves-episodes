## Summary

Show notes Published March 14, 2025, the day after lawmakers passed the state budget on the last regular day of the 2025 legislative session. Two weeks earlier, after the Legislature turned down the $825 million Lincoln County prison plan, Gov. Larry Rhoden had called a "Project Prison Reset" and said all options were on the table. Guest Steve Haugaard, attorney (Haugaard Law Office, Sioux Falls), former Speaker of the South Dakota House (2019-2020), represented District 10 in Sioux Falls from 2015 to 2023, and ran for governor in the 2022 Republican primary Background Prison: HB 1025, the $825 million, 1,500-bed men's prison planned for rural Lincoln County, failed in the 2025 session. On Feb. 27, 2025, Rhoden created the Project Prison Reset task force, chaired by Lt. Gov. Tony Venhuizen, to decide whether a new prison was needed, how big and where. Pipeline: Rhoden signed HB 1052 (Rep. Karla Lems), which bans eminent domain for carbon dioxide pipelines, on March 6, 2025. Summit Carbon Solutions then paused its South Dakota permit application. Library bill: HB 1239 would have removed the legal exemption that protects libraries and schools from obscenity prosecution. The Senate replaced it 18-16 with an appeal process (to the school or library board, then to court), and the House agreed 36-34. Rhoden signed it on March 31, 2025. Budget: Lawmakers passed a $7.3 billion budget for fiscal 2026 (SB 220) on March 13, 2025. South Dakota Searchlight reported that it includes $3.1 billion in federal funding. Update (October 2026) Prison: Rhoden's task force settled on a site in northeast Sioux Falls. In a one-day special session on Sept. 23, 2025, lawmakers approved a 1,500-bed, $650 million men's prison on Benson Road (Senate 24-11, House 51-18). The state said about $21 million spent on the Lincoln County site can't be recovered. Ground was broken on April 22, 2026, and the builders expect to finish in 2029 (the Department of Corrections lists 2030). Rhoden also set up a Correctional Rehabilitation Task Force on repeat offenses. On Oct. 29, 2026, its consultants said the state needs at least $1.9 million a year more for rehabilitation staff pay, housing and transportation help after release, and peer support, plus more officers and parole agents. The governor's office put the new prison's yearly operating cost at $44.6 million, about $20 million more than the current Sioux Falls prison operations. The final report is due Nov. 30. Pipeline: Summit never got a South Dakota permit. In March 2026, a North Dakota judge voided its North Dakota CO2 storage permit, and in May 2026 Summit said it would cut about 200 miles from the project and move its storage site to Wyoming. Steve Haugaard: In 2026 he was one of the attorneys for more than 20 rural Crooks homeowners who challenged the Sioux Falls City Council's permit for Smithfield's new pork plant in Foundation Park. On Oct. 1, 2026, Circuit Judge Ann Hoffman ruled the council hadn't followed its own ordinances, voided the permit and sent it back to the council. In the governor's race, he endorsed Toby Doeden. Gov. Rhoden won the July 28 runoff over Doeden, 83,590 to 34,435.
