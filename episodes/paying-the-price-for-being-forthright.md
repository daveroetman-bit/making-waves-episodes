## Summary

Published May 25, 2025, about 16 months after Nate Welch testified before the Legislature's Executive Board (Jan. 8, 2024) and shortly after the 2025 session passed Attorney General Marty Jackley's government accountability bills. Guest - Nate Welch, former marketing director, South Dakota Governor's Office of Economic Development (left the office in May 2023); former president and CEO of the Vermillion Chamber and Development Company; former KDLT and KSFY reporter; lives in Vermillion Background - Freedom Works Here: GOED's workforce recruitment campaign featuring Gov. Kristi Noem in high-demand jobs. The original $5 million contract went to Go West Media, a subsidiary of the Ohio-based Strategy Group, and was signed by then-GOED Commissioner Steve Westra on May 3, 2023 (Sioux Falls Live). An April 2024 amendment raised the ceiling to $9 million, with $8.5 million paid by then (South Dakota Searchlight). - The Executive Board hearing (Jan. 8, 2024): Welch told legislative leaders that GOED staff members encouraged him to allow Go West to be selected and that it was "relatively obvious" Go West was the favorite. He also said he experienced no undue pressure from GOED staff and that the award decision was not his. Lawmakers declined to call former Commissioner Westra to a later meeting, and the board adjourned without further action (Executive Board minutes). - The accountability laws Welch praises (2025 session, in effect July 1, 2025): SB 60 expanded the state auditor's access and investigative authority; SB 62 requires state employees and supervisors to report improper government conduct and crime, with supervisors reporting to the Attorney General and the Auditor General; SB 63 protects state employees who report improper government conduct or crime in good faith from retaliation. - What those laws produced: Jackley's first Public Integrity report to the Government Operations and Audit Committee (Feb. 4, 2026) said the new Public Integrity Unit reviewed 47 unique reports of misconduct by state employees in 2025, leading to 14 criminal investigations and criminal charges against four state employees (all pending at the time). - Welch's description of being told to recommend Go West and then being told to leave is his own account from this interview.
