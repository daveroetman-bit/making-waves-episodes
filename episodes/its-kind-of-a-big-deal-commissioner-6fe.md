## Summary

Published March 8, 2025, near the end of the legislative session. HB 1026 (Richmond Lake dam) had passed both chambers and was waiting for the governor, and HB 1266 (Brand Board) had just died in the Senate Agriculture and Natural Resources committee on March 6. Guest Brock Greenfield, South Dakota Commissioner of School and Public Lands (in office since January 2023; former state senator and representative, 22 years in the Legislature) The bills HB 1026 (2025): an appropriation to replace the Richmond Lake dam and spillway, about five miles northwest of Aberdeen, and for general maintenance and repair of other state-owned dams. In the conversation Greenfield puts the total price tag in the mid-$20 million range, with this bill providing the remaining roughly $13 million. HB 1266 (2025): would have attached the South Dakota State Brand Board to the Office of School and Public Lands for reporting purposes, instead of the Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources. Greenfield's office was neutral. Update (October 2026) HB 1026 passed the House 70-0 on Feb. 24, 2025 and the Senate 32-2 on Feb. 27, and Gov. Larry Rhoden signed it March 13, 2025. The Legislature's Joint Committee on Appropriations lists it as $13.05 million in general funds, on top of $6.5 million in 2022 (SB 52) and $3.15 million in general funds plus federal spending authority in 2024 (SB 70), of which the office received about $2.94 million in federal money. The mid-$20 million estimate didn't hold. After bids were opened on March 27, 2026, the project's estimated cost rose to $34,891,893. The 2026 Legislature passed SB 121, signed March 30, 2026, which moved $8,212,497 from the state's trust fund for unclaimed property to the general fund and appropriated it to Greenfield's office for the dam. The Joint Committee on Appropriations now asks the commissioner to report on construction progress and spending at its July and December meetings until the money is spent. Construction is underway. The contractor's Oct. 23, 2026 update says the seepage cutoff wall is finished, the old spillway concrete has been removed, a test section of the new stepped spillway has been poured and the lake is being held at 1,349 feet. KELOLAND News reported in July 2026 that the project is expected to be finished in June 2028. HB 1266 passed the House 47-22 on Feb. 21, 2025. In the Senate Agriculture and Natural Resources committee, a motion to pass it failed 3-4 and it was deferred to the 41st day 4-3 on March 6, 2025, which killed it. The 2026 Legislature took up other Brand Board bills, including HB 1020, which raised the maximum livestock ownership inspection fee and was signed Feb. 23, 2026, and HB 1267, which would have required statewide ownership inspection and was deferred to the 41st day in House committee. Neither moved the Brand Board to School and Public Lands. The office's facts page now lists a $275 million Permanent Trust Fund and about $19.59 million a year apportioned to school districts and endowed institutions, along with 98 state-owned dams and 5.5 million mineral acres. (In the episode Greenfield cites the fund at more than $430 million; the two figures may be measured differently.) Greenfield is running for a second term. He won the Republican nomination at the state party convention on June 27, 2026, over James Bialota, and faces Democrat Raeann Mettler on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026. His current term ends in January 2027.
