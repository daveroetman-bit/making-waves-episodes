Show notes

Recorded in early February 2026, during the South Dakota legislative session.

Guest

- Sen. Chris Karr (R-Sioux Falls), District 11, President Pro Tempore of the South Dakota Senate; he served four terms in the House before his election to the Senate in 2024

Update (October 2026)

The South Dakota Supreme Court sided with the governor. On Feb. 18 it answered Gov. Rhoden's question yes (2026 S.D. 7): the Lieutenant Governor, as President of the Senate, may cast a tie-breaking vote in the Senate on any matter, including final passage of a bill. The bill behind the fight, SB 25 (energy and transmission permit applications), was reconsidered, amended and passed 30-3 on Jan. 26 and was later signed into law.

Property taxes went the way Karr warned against. SB 245 creates a homeowner property tax reduction fund: $55.9 million went in on July 1, 2026, and starting July 1, 2027, revenue from the expiring 0.3-point sales tax cut flows in to lower school levies on owner-occupied homes. SB 96 lets counties add a sales tax to cut owner-occupied property taxes. Karr voted no on SB 96 and no on the final version of SB 245, calling it a tax shift that picks winners and losers and taxes everybody to do it. Gov. Rhoden signed both on March 12.

Karr's water bill became SB 222: $10 million, with $8 million for riparian buffer strips and $2 million for local water, wastewater and storm water projects. It passed the Senate 28-5 and the House 61-8 and was signed into law.

SB 135, the "Data Center Bill of Rights" Karr carried in the Senate with Rep. Jon Hansen, was signed into law and took effect July 1. Large data centers must pay the utility costs they cause instead of passing them to other ratepayers, and cities and counties keep the power to regulate them. The data center tax incentive bills failed.

Karr's cell phone bill, SB 198, passed the Senate 19-15 but failed in the House 28-39 on March 5. The dove bill, SB 115, passed the Senate 19-15 and then died in House Agriculture and Natural Resources, deferred to the 41st day.

Karr faces Democrat Aaron Matson for the District 11 Senate seat on Nov. 3.
