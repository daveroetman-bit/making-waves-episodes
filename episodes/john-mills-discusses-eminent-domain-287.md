## Summary

Show notes Rep. John Mills (R-Volga), District 8, joins Dave Roetman at the Cool Beans coffee shop in Brookings to talk about the carbon dioxide pipeline proposed across eastern South Dakota: where it came from, who pays for it, why so many landowners signed easements under the threat of eminent domain, and what a pipeline rupture could mean for the people who live and drive near it. He also talks about the push for a special session and his work on the Legislature's county funding study committee. Recorded in July 2023, about a week after the landowner rally at the Capitol in Pierre and the July 9 explosion at the Onida ethanol plant. Mills was serving his fourth term in the House at the time. He did not run for re-election in 2024 and left the Legislature in January 2025. Update (Oct. 2026): No special session was called in 2023; Gov. Kristi Noem said one would be "fruitless" without legislative consensus. In September 2023 the PUC denied permits for both Summit's and Navigator's pipelines, and Navigator canceled its project in October 2023. In 2024 voters rejected Referred Law 21 (SB 201), 59.4% to 40.6%, repealing the Legislature's new pipeline-siting law. In March 2025 Gov. Larry Rhoden signed HB 1052, which bans the use of eminent domain for carbon dioxide pipelines. In April 2025 the PUC denied Summit's second application, finding its route no longer viable under the new law. In May 2026 Summit announced a new core route through Nebraska to Wyoming that does not include South Dakota, though the company says it is keeping its South Dakota easements as an option for later phases.
