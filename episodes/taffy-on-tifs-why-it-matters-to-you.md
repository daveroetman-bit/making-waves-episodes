## Summary

Published Sept. 11, 2025. Recorded in early September 2025, during the 20-day petition drive that started when Rapid City published its Libertyland TIF approval. Guest Taffy Howard, Republican of Rapid City, has represented District 34 in the South Dakota Senate since January 2025. She served in the South Dakota House for District 33 from 2017 to 2023 (SD Legislature; Ballotpedia). Background How a TIF works: a tax increment financing district sets a base value for the land inside it; for a limited time, property taxes on the new value above that base are redirected to pay the district's project costs instead of going to the usual taxing bodies (SDPB, Dec. 19, 2025, quoting Rapid City's finance director). Howard's account of TIF history (created in the late 1970s), the four TIF types, how schools are held harmless, and Pennington County's $700 million in increment value is her own and was not checked for these notes. The two districts: the Rapid City Common Council approved the Destination District (Libertyland) 7-2 and the Catalyst District (a sports complex and other public infrastructure) 8-1 on Sept. 2, 2025, and Mayor Jason Salamun signed both on Sept. 4. The council had first approved them in July, but those votes were voided because of an error in a required legal notice (NewsCenter1, Sept. 4, 2025). The $125 million size of the Libertyland TIF and its roughly $47 million grant portion were also reported later (SDPB; Rapid City Post). Howard's other figures ($84 million for Catalyst, $98 million for the largest Sioux Falls TIF, $78 million for Black Hills Industrial Center, the averages for the city's other TIFs) and her accounts of the application, the project manager's remarks, the developer's paid campaign and the county commission's letter are her own. The petition: organizers turned in 314 petitions with 5,145 names, and the city confirmed 4,326 valid signatures, well over the 2,572 needed to send the Libertyland TIF to a vote (NewsCenter1, Sept. 30, 2025). Update (November 2026) The vote: in a special election on Jan. 20, 2026, Rapid City voters rejected the Destination (Libertyland) TIF district 7,965 to 3,415, about 70% to 30%, with 22% turnout (Rapid City Post; SDPB). The vote did not ban the park itself; it removed the public financing. The sports complex: the separate Catalyst TIF was not on the ballot. The city is going ahead with the youth sports complex on 22.65 acres donated by Pete Lien & Sons, paid for mainly by a hotel fee, about $20 million in tax increment financing and $5 million from the Vision Fund, with October 2028 as a possible completion target (Rapid City Business Journal, Aug. 10, 2026). State law: "Come January" turned out to be right. In the 2026 session, Howard's SB 191, which would have ended TIF grants, was tabled in Senate Taxation on Feb. 20, 2026, the same day the committee advanced a compromise, Sen. Chris Karr's SB 228. It passed 33-0 in the Senate, with Howard voting yes, and 67-0 in the House, and was signed March 12, 2026. The law raises the share of a district that must be blighted from 25% to 50%, caps TIF base values at 7.5% of a city's assessed value (10% for towns under 5,000), bans overlapping districts, requires TIF grants to be spelled out in an agreement, and requires an independent fiscal feasibility review for districts created after July 1, 2026. Howard told SDPB the Libertyland fight "spurred people to come to the table" (SDPB; SD Legislature). Election: Howard won the June 2, 2026, Republican primary for District 34 over Jason Green, 2,334 to 1,760 (57% to 43%). She faces Democrat Michael Calabrese in the general election on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026 (Ballotpedia; Pierre Review). Links SD Legislature: Sen.
