Show notes

Show notes

Published Oct. 26, 2025. Recorded in October 2025, as the Legislature's property tax task force was finishing its work ahead of the 2026 session.

Guests

Scott Odenbach (R-Spearfish) has represented District 31 in the South Dakota House since 2021 and has been House majority leader since January 2025. He is an attorney and grew up in Eureka (SD Legislature; Ballotpedia; scottodenbach.com).

Chris Karr (R-Sioux Falls) represents District 11 in the South Dakota Senate and was elected Senate president pro tempore in January 2025. He served in the House from 2017 to 2025, where he chaired House Appropriations, and he works as a financial consultant (SD Legislature; Ballotpedia).

Background

The task force they discuss is the Legislature's Comprehensive Property Tax Task Force, created by House Concurrent Resolution 6003 (2025), with Karr as chair and House Speaker Jon Hansen as vice chair. It held its last meeting Oct. 22, 2025, and its final report (Nov. 19, 2025) lists 19 recommendations, including requiring an election for school district opt-outs, making opt-outs easier to refer, tighter limits on TIF districts and a bigger income limit for the assessment freeze (SD Legislature, final report). The figures the guests give on spending growth, revenue and Medicaid costs are their own.

The prison vote: in a one-day special session on Sept. 23, 2025, lawmakers approved a $650 million, 1,500-bed men's prison in northeast Sioux Falls. The Senate passed it 24-11, exactly two-thirds, and the House 51-18 (South Dakota Searchlight, Sept. 23, 2025).

Update (November 2026)

Property taxes: Gov. Larry Rhoden signed Senate Bill 96, which lets counties adopt a sales tax of up to half a cent to cut owner-occupied property taxes, and SB 245, a state homeowner property tax relief fund, on March 12, 2026 (SDPB, March 12, 2026). Senate Bill 85, which would have sent every school district opt-out to an election, passed the Senate but failed in the House in February 2026 (SD Legislature; KOTA Radio, Feb. 5, 2026).

Data centers: Karr was the prime Senate sponsor and Odenbach a cosponsor of Senate Bill 135, the "Data Center Bill of Rights for Citizens." Signed March 24, 2026, it makes large data centers pay their own electricity costs, bars utilities from shifting those costs to residential customers, adds water-use rules and keeps local governments' power to regulate data centers (SD Legislature; South Dakota Searchlight, March 24, 2026).

Medicaid: Amendment I, on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot, would let the state end Medicaid expansion if the federal share of its cost falls below 90% (Secretary of State ballot question pamphlet).

The Republican divide: after a primary season in which 16 incumbent Republican legislators lost, Odenbach told South Dakota Searchlight in August 2026 that a "third camp" of Republicans had arisen to "trash the conservative leadership" (South Dakota Searchlight, Aug. 14, 2026). Both men were on the Nov. 3, 2026 general election ballot for their seats.
