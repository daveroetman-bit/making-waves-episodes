Show notes

Amanda Radke, a fifth-generation rancher from Mitchell and executive director of American Land & Legacy, joins Dave Roetman to talk about the new landowner coalition and the fourth property rights rally in the Capitol Rotunda the day before the 2026 session opened. She explains HJR 5001, the constitutional amendment from Rep. Spencer Gosch and Sen. Mark Lapka to bar eminent domain for private economic development, which goes back to the Kelo v. City of New London decision, and why she calls the word “solely,” added on the House floor before the resolution passed 62-5, a hostile amendment. She also covers SB 88, the “Bossly Bill” to end invasive pipeline surveys after a unanimous South Dakota Supreme Court ruling, why data centers bring property rights fights into Sioux Falls and other cities, the Maude family’s fence-line case, and how Attorney General Marty Jackley headed off a North Dakota lawsuit over HB 1052, the 2025 ban on eminent domain for carbon pipelines.

Topics: American Land & Legacy, property rights rally, HJR 5001, eminent domain, Kelo v. City of New London, “solely” amendment, SB 88, Bossly Bill, invasive surveys, data centers, local control, Maude family fence case, HB 1052, carbon pipelines, Marty Jackley, H.R. 4135

“George Washington said it best, that freedom and property rights go hand in hand. You can’t have one without the other. And your American dream, your ability to own property, whether that’s land or a home, is a fundamental God-given inherent right that’s protected under the Constitution.” - Amanda Radke
