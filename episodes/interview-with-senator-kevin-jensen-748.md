## Summary

Show notes Published July 26, 2025, less than three weeks after Gov. Larry Rhoden's Project Prison Reset task force recommended a $650 million, 1,500-bed men's prison in Sioux Falls and before the special session that voted on it. This is part 2 of a series; part 1 (May 31, 2025) covers the failed Lincoln County plan, maximum-security beds and Metal Craft Industries. Guest Sen. Kevin Jensen, R-Canton, South Dakota Senate District 16; six years on the state Corrections Commission; co-chair, with Rep. Greg Jamison, of the Legislature's 2025 interim study committee on Initial Incarceration, Reentry Analysis, and Comparison of Relevant States (South Dakota Legislative Research Council) Background July 8, 2025: the Project Prison Reset task force voted 22-0 to recommend a 1,500-bed men's prison in northeast Sioux Falls, with 1,200 beds for higher-security inmates and 300 for lower-security inmates, at $650 million. The state's consultants had priced that design at $725 million. Task force members asked about Nebraska's cheaper prison; the state engineer said Nebraska had refused to release its specifications, citing nondisclosure agreements with its contractors (South Dakota Searchlight; The Dakota Scout). Nebraska: Hausmann Construction of Lincoln was the low bidder, at $313.3 million, for Nebraska's 1,512-bed multi-custody prison replacing the Nebraska State Penitentiary; the Nebraska Legislature had appropriated $350 million (Nebraska Department of Correctional Services, June 18, 2025). The $366 million figure in this interview is Jensen's. The $525 million estimate: Indiana-based Elevatus Architecture told Attorney General Marty Jackley in a letter that a similar 1,500-bed prison could cost closer to $525 million than $825 million (Dakota News Network, June 3, 2025). Jensen refers to it in this interview as $535 million. The Huron company: Trussbilt, headquartered in Huron, makes security wall panels, detention doors and modular steel prison cells; Huron's development corporation pitched its cells to the state in an April 14, 2025, response to the state's prison site request (Greater Huron Development Corporation). The officer assault Jensen mentions: inmates Lester Monroe and Kyle Jones were found guilty in November 2024 of attempted first-degree murder of a law enforcement officer and aggravated assault for the Aug. 24, 2023, attack on two correctional officers in Unit D of the Jameson Annex (South Dakota Attorney General; SDPB). Figures Jensen gives in this interview are his own: a recidivism rate of 40 to 44 percent for about 20 years, with 28 percent technical parole violations and 15 percent new crimes; about 150 absconders; 230 inmates qualified for work release "at the minimum center" with six to nine working, and 345 in Rapid City with one working; 57 parolees per parole officer; about $35,000 a year to hold one inmate; and the 65-year sentence for a $150,000 financial crime. Update (November 2026) Sept. 23, 2025: in a special session, the Legislature approved the 1,500-bed, $650 million men's prison in northeast Sioux Falls, about 3 miles from the penitentiary. Jensen voted no, saying a new building won't fix the policies and the lack of programming inside the prisons. Corrections Secretary Kellie Wasko had announced her resignation earlier that month (SDPB, South Dakota Searchlight, Sioux Falls Live). April 22, 2026: the state broke ground on the new prison. The contractor said it is on track to come in under its $650 million guaranteed maximum price, with completion expected in 2029 (South Dakota Searchlight). The Department of Corrections lists 2030. Aug. 5, 2026: the penitentiary lost its fourth warden in five years in a senior staff shake-up under Corrections Secretary Nick Lamb (South Dakota Searchlight). Aug. 19, 2026: a Council of State Governments Justice Center reentry assessment for Gov. Rhoden's Correctional Rehabilitation Task Force found that South Dakota's recidivism rate is 50 percent (half of inmates released return within three years), that 35 percent of people released in 2025 had no programming, that people often leave prison without IDs and other vital documents, and that the state has no data on whether released inmates find and keep jobs (South Dakota Searchlight). Oct. 4, 2026: the new $87 million, 288-bed women's prison in Rapid City began taking inmates in September and held 79 women at the end of the month (South Dakota Searchlight). Oct. 29, 2026: consultants told the Correctional Rehabilitation Task Force that it will cost at least $1.9 million a year to start fixing rehabilitation in the prison system. A Rhoden policy adviser estimated the new men's prison will cost $44.6 million a year to run, about $20 million more than current Sioux Falls prison operations. Jensen had warned in September 2025 of about $30 million in added operating costs (his estimate). The task force's final report is due Nov. 30 (South Dakota Searchlight, Hub City Radio/KELO). Jensen is unopposed for re-election to Senate District 16 on Nov. 3, 2026 (Ballotpedia).
