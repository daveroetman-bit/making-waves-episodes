Show notes

Sen. Kevin Jensen of District 16, who has chaired Health and Human Services in the South Dakota House and Senate for eight years, joins Dave Roetman to explain Amendment I. The Legislature placed it on the ballot, and it would end expanded Medicaid coverage if the federal share of funding drops below 90%. Jensen explains the FMAP federal match: about 51% federal for traditional Medicaid and 90% for expansion under the Affordable Care Act. He says the state’s share has grown from $18 million to about $34 million, and that losing the higher match would cost South Dakota well over $100 million a year. He argues nothing changes while Washington keeps its promise and urges a yes vote on I.

Topics: Amendment I, Medicaid expansion, FMAP, Affordable Care Act, 90% federal match, state budget, federal funding, teacher pay, Attorney General interpretation, 2026 midterms

Sen. Kevin Jensen of District 16 has chaired Health and Human Services in both the South Dakota House and Senate for a total of eight years. He joins Dave to make the case for Amendment I, the constitutional amendment the Legislature put on the ballot. It would end expanded Medicaid coverage if the federal government drops its share of funding below 90%.

Jensen explains the federal match: traditional Medicaid splits roughly 51% federal and 49% state, while expansion under the Affordable Care Act is 90% federal and 10% state. South Dakota’s share has doubled in two years, from $18 million in the first budget to about $34 million now, with total spending near $340 million. He says Congress debated cutting the expansion match back to the traditional rate in 2025. If that happened, the state would have to find well over $100 million a year, in a Legislature that fights over $5 and $10 million items at the end of every session. That money would come from teacher pay, state employees and other priorities.

Jensen’s bottom line is that nothing changes as long as Washington keeps its 90% promise. Amendment I only kicks in if the federal government backs out, and it protects South Dakota taxpayers from hundreds of millions of dollars in exposure. He’s a firm yes on I.

“If the federal government doesn’t change, nothing happens. It stays the same. You can sleep at night knowing that as long as the federal government holds their 90 percent, nothing’s going to happen.”

“This amendment only kicks in if the federal government backs out. So you can blame the federal government, not your state.”

Read the official text of Amendment I (HJR 5001) and the Attorney General's explanation on the South Dakota Secretary of State's website.
