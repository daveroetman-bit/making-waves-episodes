## Summary

Amanda Radke, a fifth-generation rancher from Mitchell and executive director of American Land & Legacy, joins Dave Roetman to talk about the new landowner coalition and the fourth property rights rally in the Capitol Rotunda the day before the 2026 session opened. She explains HJR 5001, the constitutional amendment from Rep. Spencer Gosch and Sen. Mark Lapka to bar eminent domain for private economic development, which goes back to the Kelo v. City of New London decision, and why she calls the word “solely,” added on the House floor before the resolution passed 62-5, a hostile amendment. She also covers SB 88, the “Bossly Bill” to end invasive pipeline surveys after a unanimous South Dakota Supreme Court ruling, why data centers bring property rights fights into Sioux Falls and other cities, the Maude family’s fence-line case, and how Attorney General Marty Jackley headed off a North Dakota lawsuit over HB 1052, the 2025 ban on eminent domain for carbon pipelines. The House-floor amendment that put “solely” back in (HJR5001C, listed in the Legislature’s record as requested by Rep. Greg Jamison) passed 35-32 on Jan. 27, just before the 62-5 vote. Senate State Affairs rewrote it on Feb. 25 (Radke testified for it), dropping the ban on taking land for a private party’s economic development and instead saying public use does not include a bigger tax base, more tax revenue, jobs or general economic health, and sent it on 5-3. If a landowner says no, a company with eminent domain power may now do only a minimally invasive “examination,” not a survey, and only with a pending or approved PUC siting permit application, 30 days’ written notice and payment for any damage.
