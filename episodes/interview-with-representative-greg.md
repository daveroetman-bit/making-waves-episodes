## Summary

Show notes Published Oct. 11, 2025. Recorded at a Sioux Falls coffee shop in the weeks after the Sept. 23, 2025, special session on the new men's prison. Guest Greg Jamison, Republican of Sioux Falls, represents District 12 in the South Dakota House, where he has served since 2021. He is a real estate and marketing professional, a former Sioux Falls city councilor and a two-time candidate for mayor of Sioux Falls. In 2025 he served on the Project Prison Reset task force and the Comprehensive Property Tax Task Force, and co-chaired the Legislature's summer study on incarceration and re-entry (Ballotpedia; The Dakota Scout, Jan. 5, 2026; SD Legislature). Background The prison: on Sept. 23, 2025, in a one-day special session, the Legislature passed Senate Bill 2, a $650 million, 1,500-bed men's prison in northeast Sioux Falls, 24-11 in the Senate and 51-18 in the House. Gov. Larry Rhoden signed it that evening (South Dakota Searchlight; SD Legislature bill status). The "gym full of angry people" he describes was a June 19, 2025, public forum at Worthing Elementary on Worthing as a possible prison site; Worthing's mayor noted Jamison was there to listen (Dakota News Now, June 19, 2025). Jamison's account of the deal-making and the location's role in the vote is his own. The summer study Jamison co-chaired with Sen. Kevin Jensen was the Initial Incarceration, Reentry Analysis, and Comparison of Relevant States Interim Committee. It met June 24, July 16 and Oct. 29, 2025, and compared South Dakota with Montana, North Dakota and Wyoming (SD Legislature press release, June 18, 2025; final report, Nov. 19, 2025). His figure that Native Americans make up 40% of the prison population is his own. The "new task force the governor's just doing" is the Correctional Rehabilitation Task Force, created by Executive Order 2025-07 the day SB 2 was signed. Rhoden named its 30 members, Jamison among them, on Oct. 7, 2025; it is chaired by Lt. Gov. Tony Venhuizen and looks at programming, faith-based and Native American-focused programs and re-entry (Gov. Rhoden press release, Oct. 7, 2025; South Dakota Searchlight, Oct. 7, 2025). The property tax task force he mentions is the Comprehensive Property Tax Task Force. At its final meeting in Pierre on Oct. 22, 2025, it approved 19 recommendations for the Legislature's Executive Board. One matched the QR-code idea Jamison describes here: put a web link and QR code on every tax bill that takes the taxpayer to a Department of Revenue site with more detail. Another let owners apply for property tax relief programs online (SD Legislature final report, Nov. 19, 2025). City elections: 2025's House Bill 1130 moved even-year municipal and school board elections to the June primary or November general election date starting in 2026, so Sioux Falls voted for mayor on June 2, 2026, instead of in April (SD Legislature; Sioux Falls Live). Update (November 2026) The tax-bill transparency idea became law. Jamison chaired House Local Government, which introduced House Bill 1258. It requires every property tax bill to carry a QR code and web address linking to a Department of Revenue site on property taxes and relief programs. It passed the House 60-2 and the Senate 34-0 and was signed March 12, 2026 (SD Legislature session law, Chapter 49; SDPB, March 12, 2026). A companion bill he presented, HB 1260, lets cities set up property tax rebate programs; it was signed March 4, 2026 (SD Legislature). The re-entry study's final report (Nov. 19, 2025) recommended more GED and technical education in prison, restoring the inmate community service program and substance abuse, mental health and faith-based counseling, and letting nonprofits run residential programming (SD Legislature). April 22, 2026: ground was broken on the new men's prison at 3400 N. Bahnson Ave. in Sioux Falls; completion is expected in 2029 (South Dakota Searchlight). Jamison decided to run. He did not seek a fourth House term and entered the Sioux Falls mayor's race in January 2026 (The Dakota Scout, Jan. 5, 2026). He finished third on June 2 with 7,081 votes (18.3%), then endorsed Jamie Smith in the runoff; Christine Erickson won the June 23 runoff by four votes after a recount (SDPB, June 4, 2026; Wikipedia). He said he has no plans to run for the Legislature again (SDPB). He serves in the House through the end of his term in January 2027.
