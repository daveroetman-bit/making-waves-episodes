Show notes

Published Dec. 20, 2025. Recorded in late 2025 at Call to Freedom in Sioux Falls, a few weeks after the group opened its Rapid City office, while volunteers wrapped Christmas presents in the next room.

Guests

Becky Rasmussen founded Call to Freedom as its own nonprofit after a 2015 outreach at the Sturgis rally, where she met a teenage girl named Marissa who had been re-recruited into trafficking. She filed for 501(c)(3) status in November 2015, received approval in January 2016 and opened the doors that March. She is Call to Freedom's president and CEO (Call to Freedom).

Rachel Foltz was Call to Freedom's director of impact and program relations when this episode was recorded and leads its public policy work. Her title is now director of program and prevention services (Call to Freedom; Dakota News Now).

Background

Call to Freedom is a faith-based 501(c)(3) nonprofit in Sioux Falls that serves victims and survivors of sex trafficking, labor trafficking and commercial sexual exploitation through what it calls a continuum of care: safety planning, housing, transportation, medical care, counseling, addiction recovery, education and job help, and legal advocacy. It has nearly 30 full-time staff and offices in Sioux Falls and Rapid City (Call to Freedom; KOTA).

Marissa's House, named for the girl Rasmussen met in Sturgis, is a secure 12-unit apartment complex (six one-bedroom, three two-bedroom and three three-bedroom units) where survivors live with their children while they get support (Call to Freedom).

Call to Freedom served 527 people in 2024, more than in any year since its founding, including 92 survivors of technology-facilitated abuse. It received a 2024 Bush Prize for Community Innovation (Call to Freedom 2024 annual report).

The legislation from Attorney General Marty Jackley that Rasmussen mentions is Senate Bill 58 (2025). It broadened the definition of coercion, made clear that trafficking a minor does not require proof of force, fraud or coercion, made it a crime to profit from a trafficking venture or to obstruct trafficking enforcement, and passed 35-0 in the Senate and 70-0 in the House. Gov. Larry Rhoden signed it on March 12, 2025, and it took effect July 1, 2025 (SD Legislature; Attorney General's office).

The federal Crime Victims Fund, created by the Victims of Crime Act of 1984, is paid for by fines and penalties from federal criminal cases, not tax dollars, and is passed to the states as grants (U.S. Office for Victims of Crime). South Dakota victim service groups say their federal VOCA money in fiscal 2026 is still about 20% below 2023 levels, and that since 2023 the state has approved its own money each year to make up part of the loss (South Dakota Network Against Family Violence and Sexual Assault).

Before 2026, South Dakota let 16- and 17-year-olds marry with a parent's or guardian's permission, the law since 1993 (South Dakota News Watch).

Update (November 2026)

App store accountability: House Bill 1275 (2026), with Rep. John Hughes as prime sponsor and Sen. Sue Peterson carrying it in the Senate, would have required app stores to verify users' ages and get a parent's consent before a minor downloads an app. Foltz testified for it on behalf of Call to Freedom. It passed the House 50-17 on Feb. 20, 2026, but the Senate State Affairs Committee killed it 5-4 on March 2, 2026 (SD Legislature; South Dakota Searchlight).

Victim services funding: House Bill 1200 (2026), with Reps. Tim Reed and Taylor Rehfeldt as prime sponsors, puts $5.5 million from the state general fund toward Department of Public Safety grants for nonprofits that serve abused or neglected children and victims of domestic violence, sexual assault and sex trafficking or commercial sexual exploitation. The final version passed the House 58-8 and the Senate 31-2 on March 11, 2026, was signed by Gov. Rhoden in March and took effect June 30, 2026. Grant applications are taken each July and August (SD Legislature; South Dakota Network Against Family Violence and Sexual Assault).

Child marriage: Senate Bill 159 (2026), with Sen. Tom Pischke as prime sponsor, makes 18 the general minimum age to marry. Sixteen- and 17-year-olds can still marry, but only with a circuit court order or notarized consent from both parents or a legal guardian, and only if the two people are no more than four years apart in age. A judge must hold an in-person hearing, meet privately with each applicant and make written findings, and the license must wait 30 days after a judge approves. It passed the Senate 25-8 and the House 40-27, the Senate agreed to the House changes 31-3, and Gov. Rhoden signed it in March 2026. (SD Legislature).

Call to Freedom held a free "Keeping Kids Safe Online" training for parents and caregivers in Sioux Falls on Nov. 3, 2026, with Avera Health and Homeland Security Investigations, on sextortion, parental controls and the Take It Down tool (Dakota News Now; KOTA).

The next informational luncheon is Nov. 19, 2026, at 11:45 a.m. at the Life 96.5 radio station, 4721 W. 71st St., Sioux Falls, and on Zoom. Registration is on the Call to Freedom events page (Call to Freedom).

Anyone who needs help can call Call to Freedom at (605) 759-3565 (Call to Freedom).
