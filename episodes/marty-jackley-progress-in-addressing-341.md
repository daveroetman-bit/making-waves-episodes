## Summary

Show notes Published April 26, 2025, a few weeks after the 2025 legislative session and after a Hughes County jury convicted former Child Protection Services employee Lonna Carroll of stealing about $1.78 million from the state (April 3, 2025). All six of Attorney General Marty Jackley's 2025 bills had been signed by Gov. Larry Rhoden by March 12, 2025. Guest Marty Jackley, South Dakota Attorney General (appointed in 2009 and served through 2019; elected again in 2022 and sworn in in January 2023), U.S. Attorney for South Dakota from 2006 to 2009 Background Government accountability package (2025): SB 60 gave the state auditor access to the state accounting system and more investigative authority; SB 61 gave the Board of Internal Control rulemaking authority; SB 63 protects state employees who report improper government conduct or crime; SB 62 requires supervisors to report it to the Attorney General and the Auditor General. Jackley wanted a felony for supervisors who stay silent. The House voted 38-31 for the governor's misdemeanor version, and the Senate agreed 20-14 on March 5, 2025. All six bills took effect July 1, 2025. Human trafficking and prisons (2025): SB 58 rewrote the human trafficking law, defined coercion, made obstructing trafficking enforcement a crime and set mandatory minimum sentences (at least 15 years for a first offense of first-degree trafficking). SB 59 raised penalties for delivering or possessing contraband, such as cell phones and drugs, in a state prison. The theft cases: Lonna Carroll was convicted on April 3, 2025, of stealing $1,777,665.73 from Child Protection Services through 215 fraudulent requests between 2010 and 2023. Other cases included a health inspector charged with billing the state for food-safety inspections that never happened, and vehicle-title cases at the Department of Revenue: Lynne Hunsley got probation in December 2024, and Sandra O'Day died of cancer before any charges were filed. Update (October 2026) The theft case: On July 1, 2025, Circuit Judge Christina Klinger sentenced Lonna Carroll to 13 years in prison with six suspended and ordered her to repay the full $1,777,665.73. More transparency laws: In the 2026 session, all 10 of Jackley's bills passed and were signed, and they took effect July 1, 2026. They include three open-meeting bills (SB 46, detailed agendas with a misdemeanor penalty; SB 47, stricter rules for executive sessions; SB 48, agendas posted online 72 hours ahead), SB 44, which lets the Attorney General seek court-approved subpoenas for business records in internet crimes against children and human trafficking investigations, and SB 42, tougher penalties for drugs in state prisons. Human trafficking: In the 14th annual ICAC operation at the Sturgis Rally (Aug. 7-12, 2026), six men were arrested, two of them on first-degree human trafficking charges. The Attorney General's Office says the rally operation has led to about 104 arrests since it began. Marty Jackley: He ran for South Dakota's U.S. House seat after Rep. Dusty Johnson ran for governor. He won the June 2, 2026, Republican primary with 79.2% of the vote over James Bialota, and he faces Democrat Nikki Gronli in the Nov. 3, 2026, general election. Links Gov.
