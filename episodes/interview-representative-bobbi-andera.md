## Summary

Published Sept. 9, 2025. Recorded in early September 2025, about two weeks before the Sept. 23 special session on the prison. Guest Bobbi Andera, Republican of Sioux Falls, has represented District 10 in the South Dakota House since January 2025. She is a U.S. Air Force veteran and lists her work as entrepreneur and scientist. She serves on the House Health and Human Services, Local Government, and Military and Veterans' Affairs committees, and on the Medical Marijuana Oversight Committee (SD Legislature; Ballotpedia). Background The Medical Marijuana Oversight Committee was created by Initiated Measure 26, the medical marijuana law voters passed in 2020. In 2025 it was chaired by Rep. Josephine Garcia, with Andera, Sen. John Carley and Sen. Lauren Nelson as the legislative members. At its first 2025 meeting, the Department of Health reported 13,453 registered patients and 66 dispensaries, and law enforcement members described the trouble with unregulated THC products sold in gas stations and other stores. In November the committee recommended that THC products be sold only in licensed medical marijuana facilities, not in stores, that THC levels in medical products be capped, and that an interim committee study illegal THC sales at smoke shops (2025 final report). The "farm bill" products: the 2018 federal farm bill legalized hemp with up to 0.3% delta-9 THC, which let intoxicating hemp-derived products such as delta-8 THC be sold (Congressional Research Service). The prison: Corrections Secretary Kellie Wasko announced her resignation on Sept. 2, 2025, a day after 20 House Republicans signed a letter asking Gov. Larry Rhoden to "clean up his Department of Corrections" before the special session (South Dakota Searchlight; The Dakota Scout). Dave's "19" and the counts of legislators who might have signed are the speakers' own estimates. On Sept. 23, 2025, the Legislature approved a 1,500-bed, $650 million men's prison on Benson Road in northeast Sioux Falls, 24-11 in the Senate and 51-18 in the House. Rhoden signed it that night and created a Correctional Rehabilitation Task Force (South Dakota Searchlight; SDPB). Andera voted no (Capitol Lens). Dave's figures for the Jameson Annex (built in 1993, 540 maximum-security beds, fewer than 200 maximum-security inmates) are his own and were not checked for these notes; so is Andera's 45% recidivism figure. SB 73 (2015) overhauled juvenile justice, expanding community-based programs and diversion and limiting state commitment to higher-risk youth, starting in 2016 (SD Legislature; Pew). The state's STAR Academy juvenile campus near Custer closed in April 2016 as the number of committed youth fell (Mitchell Republic). Andera's account of how the law affected schools is her own. Update (November 2026) Prison: construction of the Sioux Falls prison is under way. Nick Lamb was named corrections secretary in October 2025 (Dakota News Now). On Oct. 29, 2026, the governor's Correctional Rehabilitation Task Force heard its consultants' final recommendations: at least $1.9 million a year for rehabilitation staff pay, housing, transportation and peer support for people leaving prison, in a state where half of former inmates commit a new crime within three years. Its final report is due Nov. 30 (South Dakota Searchlight, Oct. 29, 2026). Her bill: Andera's HB 1068 in the 2026 session would have let pharmacists dispense ivermectin and hydroxychloroquine to adults under a written protocol from a health care provider, with liability protection. It cleared House Health and Human Services but failed on the House floor 28-40 on Feb. 5, 2026 (SD Legislature; DRG News). Hemp: Andera co-sponsored SB 61, a 2026 bill to ban hemp-derived intoxicants sold for non-medical use; it failed in the Senate 14-19 (SD Legislature). Congress acted instead: a November 2025 spending law narrows the federal definition of hemp. Synthetic cannabinoids lose hemp status on Nov. 12, 2026, and the rest, including a 0.4 milligram total-THC limit per container, takes effect Dec. 11, 2026, after a delay passed in September (Congressional Research Service, Sept. 28, 2026). Election: Andera ran for a second term but finished third in the June 2, 2026, Republican primary for District 10's two House seats, with 31.7% to John Pohlman's 34.9% and Kimberly Petterson's 33.4%. She is not on the Nov. 3, 2026, ballot; Pohlman and Petterson face Democrats Adam Bair and Kathleen Brudvig. Her House term ends in January 2027 (Ballotpedia). Links SD Legislature: Rep.
