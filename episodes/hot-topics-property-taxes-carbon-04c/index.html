Show notes

Recorded in early February 2025, during the 100th South Dakota legislative session, shortly after the House passed HB 1052 (49-19 on Jan. 27) and the House Education Committee killed the two school choice bills (Jan. 29).

Guest

Rep. Aaron Aylward (R), South Dakota House of Representatives, District 6, a leader of the South Dakota Freedom Caucus

Update (October 2026)

Property taxes: Gov. Larry Rhoden signed SB 216 on March 13, 2025. It caps growth in owner-occupied assessments at 3% countywide for five years, caps new-construction budget growth at 3%, and raises the income and home-value limits for the elderly and disabled assessment freeze. The Legislature also created the summer task force Aylward described (HCR 6003), the Comprehensive Property Tax Task Force, which met from May to October 2025 and sent 19 recommendations to the 2026 session. On March 12, 2026, Rhoden signed SB 96 and SB 245, which he called the largest property tax cut in state history. SB 245 created a homeowner property tax reduction fund, seeded with about $55.9 million on July 1, 2026, to lower school levies on owner-occupied homes; starting in August 2027 it is funded by 0.3 percentage points of the state sales tax as the temporary 2023 sales tax cut expires. SB 96 lets each county add up to a half-cent sales tax, with every dollar credited against owner-occupied property taxes. Critics call it a tax shift onto sales tax; the school funding formula itself has not been replaced.

Carbon pipeline: Rep. Karla Lems' HB 1052 passed the Senate 23-12 on March 4, 2025, and Rhoden signed it on March 6, 2025. South Dakota now bars eminent domain for any pipeline whose main purpose is carrying carbon oxide. Sen. John Carley's broader SB 49 (wind, solar and hydrogen too) was deferred to the 41st day in Senate State Affairs, 7-1, on Feb. 19, 2025. In May 2026 Summit Carbon Solutions announced a new route west through Nebraska to Wyoming that leaves South Dakota out, though the company says its South Dakota easements remain "valuable optionality for future phases."

Prison: The $825 million Lincoln County plan died in the House in February 2025. After the governor's Project Prison Reset task force, lawmakers approved a 1,500-bed, $650 million men's prison in northeast Sioux Falls in a Sept. 23, 2025 special session.

Education savings accounts: HB 1020 (2025) was deferred to the 41st day in House Education, 9-6, on Jan. 29, 2025. No ESA program has been enacted.
