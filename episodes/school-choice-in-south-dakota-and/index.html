Show notes

Show notes

Published Oct. 12, 2025. Recorded in Sioux Falls in the fall of 2025, during the federal government shutdown that began Oct. 1, 2025.

Guest

Jennifer Beving was deputy state director of Americans for Prosperity South Dakota (AFP-SD) when this was recorded; she joined AFP-SD in that role in 2024. She grew up on a farm in eastern South Dakota (Clark), lives in the Black Hills, is a licensed attorney who practiced criminal defense, and left her law practice to homeschool her children (AFP-SD team page; AFP-SD press release, Jan. 7, 2026).

Background

The homeschool law she helped write and lobby is 2021's Senate Bill 177, which rewrote South Dakota's alternative instruction (homeschool) law. It simplified the yearly notice to the school district, dropped the standardized testing requirement and let homeschool students take part in school activities. It passed the House 41-27, and Gov. Kristi Noem signed it in March 2021; it took effect July 1, 2021 (Associated School Boards of South Dakota, March 13, 2021). Her account of how the bill came about and of the committee testimony is her own.

School choice in Pierre: the 2025 session's two education savings account bills both failed in House Education on Jan. 29, 2025. HB 1020, backed by Gov. Larry Rhoden, would have given up to $3,000 per student for private school, homeschool or other costs; it was deferred 9-6, and HB 1009 failed 8-7 (South Dakota Searchlight, Jan. 29, 2025; SD Legislature). Her statements that South Dakota has no charter school law and is "one of the last two states" without one, and the figures she gives on spending and enrollment, are her own.

Opt-outs: a school board can impose an opt-out (an excess property tax levy) with a two-thirds vote of the board. Voters can refer it to an election with a petition signed by at least 5% of the district's registered voters, filed within 20 days of the first publication of the decision (SDCL 10-12-43; other taxing districts: SDCL 10-13-36). This is the 20-day window Dave describes.

The "Your Town, Your Taxes" tour she previews went ahead in November 2025, with dinners in Sioux Falls (Nov. 4), Rapid City (Nov. 6), Spearfish (Nov. 13), Custer and Aberdeen. AFP-SD's property tax agenda calls for "truth in taxation" notices and levy limits (AFP-SD Tax Tour page).

Update (November 2026)

On Jan. 7, 2026, AFP-SD named Beving its state director (AFP-SD press release). In January 2026 AFP-SD hosted more than two dozen lawmakers at a dinner on the REINS Act, its bill to require legislative approval of costly new regulations (AFP press release, Jan. 21, 2026).

Property taxes: over Presidents Day weekend in February 2026, AFP-SD held property tax town halls in Sioux Falls and Dakota Dunes with Gov. Rhoden (AFP press release; Dakota News Now, Feb. 14, 2026). The governor's Senate Bill 96, which lets counties adopt a sales tax of up to half a cent to cut owner-occupied property taxes, was signed March 12, 2026, along with SB 245, a state homeowner property tax relief fund (SDPB, March 12, 2026).

School choice: Senate Bill 84, signed Feb. 6, 2026, expanded the state's Partners in Education tax credit scholarship by raising the income limit and the scholarship cap (Gov. Rhoden press release, Feb. 6, 2026; SD Legislature). On Nov. 14, 2025, Rhoden opted South Dakota into the new federal scholarship tax credit from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Starting Jan. 1, 2027, donors can get a federal tax credit of up to $1,700 ($3,400 for married couples) for gifts to approved scholarship organizations (Gov. Rhoden press release, Nov. 14, 2025; IRS; SD Partners in Education).
