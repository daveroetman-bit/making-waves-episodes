## Summary

Show notes Recorded in January 2025, as the Legislature was taking up House Bill 1025, the funding bill for the Lincoln County prison. Doug Weber had just released a letter to legislators asking them to pause the project. boondoggle (noun): a wasteful and worthless project undertaken for political, corporate, or personal gain, typically a government project funded by taxpayers. Is the proposed 1,500-bed prison in Lincoln County a boondoggle? Guest Doug Weber, former chief warden and director of adult corrections, South Dakota Department of Corrections (1981-2013) Update (October 2026) The Lincoln County prison was never built. On Feb. 21, 2025, the South Dakota House voted 34-35 against a stripped-down version of HB 1025 that would only have moved $148.1 million into the prison fund, and a motion to reconsider failed on a 35-35 tie on Feb. 24. On Feb. 27, Gov. Larry Rhoden conceded the $825 million plan was dead and created the Project Prison Reset task force, chaired by Lt. Gov. Tony Venhuizen. Weber told South Dakota Searchlight that day, "Still have a little work to do, but the heavy lift is done." The task force voted to drop the Lincoln County site in June 2025 and recommended a 1,500-bed prison in northeast Sioux Falls capped at $650 million. In a special session on Sept. 23, 2025, lawmakers passed it (SB 2) with exactly the two-thirds needed in the Senate (24-11) and 51-18 in the House. The state trades the Lincoln County land to the owner of the new Benson Road site. Of the $52 million spent on the Lincoln County plan, the governor's office says $21 million can't be recovered. The new prison is still a 1,500-bed, $650 million project and the most expensive capital project in state history. Ground was broken on April 22, 2026, and completion is expected in 2029. It replaces the oldest parts of the penitentiary on the Hill. Corrections Secretary Kellie Wasko announced on Sept. 2, 2025 that she was resigning, and left on Oct. 20, 2025. Nick Lamb now leads the Department of Corrections.
