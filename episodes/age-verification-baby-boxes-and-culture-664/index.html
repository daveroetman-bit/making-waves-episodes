Show notes

Show notes

Recorded around Feb. 6, 2025, during the 100th South Dakota legislative session, the day after the deadline to file bills. At the time HB 1053 and HB 1044 had passed the House and were waiting for Senate hearings, and HB 1239 had just been filed.

Guest

Rep. Bethany Soye (R), South Dakota House of Representatives, District 9, House majority whip (2025-2026)

Update (October 2026)

All three bills became law, and all three took effect July 1, 2025.

HB 1053 (age verification): It passed the House 61-5 and the Senate 34-0, and Gov. Larry Rhoden signed it on Feb. 27, 2025. Sites with material harmful to minors must use reasonable age verification and can't keep or sell the ID data. The attorney general enforces it, with no private lawsuits. On June 27, 2025, the U.S. Supreme Court upheld Texas's similar law 6-3 in Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton, and Attorney General Marty Jackley said the ruling backs South Dakota's law. Pornhub's parent company cut off South Dakota rather than comply.

HB 1044 (Baby Gabriel bill): It passed the House 68-0 and the Senate 35-0, and was signed March 6, 2025. South Dakota's first Safe Haven Baby Box opened in February 2026 at Rapid City Fire Station 1, after work with South Dakota Right to Life. It's the privately funded, locally driven model Soye described.

HB 1239 (library books): It passed the House 38-32. In the Senate, Sen. David Wheeler's amendment (18-16) kept the affirmative defense for schools and libraries. In its place it requires every public school and library policy to let anyone appeal to the school board or library board on whether material is obscene, with that decision open to review in court. The House agreed to the Senate version 36-34, with Soye voting no, and it was signed in March 2025.

In 2026, an app-store age verification bill, HB 1275 (prime sponsor Rep. John Hughes, cosponsored by Soye), passed the House but was deferred to the 41st day in Senate State Affairs on March 2, 2026.

Rep. Soye withdrew her 2026 reelection petition and was not on the June 2, 2026 primary ballot. Her term runs through January 2027.
