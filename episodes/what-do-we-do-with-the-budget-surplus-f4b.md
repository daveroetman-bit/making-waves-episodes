## Summary

Show notes Rep. Marty Overweg (R-District 21) joins Dave Roetman to talk about South Dakota's roughly $100 million budget surplus and what to do about foreign ownership of farmland. Overweg says there are only two ways to get a surplus, federal money or overtaxing, and that the state is overtaxing its citizens. He wants the sales tax cut taken the rest of the way down to 4% and says the money is there to take the sales tax off food. On foreign ownership, he talks about the governor's committee and an informal group of six farm organizations working on a better approach after the governor's 2023 bill failed, and he warns that South Dakota agriculture is too intertwined with foreign seed companies, packers and suppliers to just shut everything down. Recorded in summer 2023. Update (Oct. 2026): The 2023 cut Overweg co-sponsored (HB 1137) lowered the state sales tax from 4.5% to 4.2%, but only through June 30, 2027. A 2026 bill to make it permanent (SB 195, which he also co-sponsored) died in the Senate on a 17-17 tie and failed again on reconsideration, 16-17. Lawmakers instead passed SB 245, which sends the 0.3-point increase to a new homeowner property tax reduction fund when the rate goes back to 4.5% in July 2027. In November 2024, voters rejected Initiated Measure 28, which would have ended the state sales tax on food and other items sold for human consumption, 69% to 31%. On farmland, the Legislature passed HB 1231 in 2024, and Gov. Noem signed it that March. It bans people, companies and governments from China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia and Venezuela from owning South Dakota ag land, with narrow lease exceptions, and in 2026 SB 40 added a penalty for failing to report foreign ownership. Overweg is now the House assistant majority leader. He advanced from the June 2, 2026, Republican primary, and he and Jim Halverson are the only candidates on the Nov. 3 ballot for District 21's two House seats. (Sources are linked below.) Links Rep.
