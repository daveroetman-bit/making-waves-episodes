Show notes

Show notes

Published Nov. 18, 2025. Recorded in November 2025 at the Makers Exchange in Tea, a shop that sells goods from more than 100 local businesses, a few days after Gov. Larry Rhoden opted South Dakota in to the federal scholarship tax credit (Nov. 14, 2025).

Guest

Tesa Schwans (R) represents District 9, which covers northwest Sioux Falls, Hartford and Wall Lake, in the South Dakota House. She was elected in 2024 and took office in January 2025. She is a licensed cosmetologist, a longtime salon owner in Sioux Falls and a regional sales representative, and she serves on the House Commerce and Energy and Education committees (SD Legislature; tesaforsd.com).

Background

The Teacher Compensation Review Board met three times in summer 2025 (June 16, July 28 and Aug. 20), with Schwans as a member. Its final recommendations were to leave unchanged the minimum teacher salary and average compensation targets set in 2024, to have the Department of Education expand training on handling student behavior, to collect one-time data on class time lost to athletics and activities, and to write a white paper on smartphones in schools (2025 TCRB report; DOE responses).

The Teacher Apprenticeship Pathway, launched in fall 2023, lets paraprofessionals (teacher's aides) earn a bachelor's degree and teaching certificate in about two years while they keep working in their own school district. Dakota State University teaches the elementary and special education track and Northern State the secondary track, and apprentices pay about $1,000 a year. Dr. David De Jong is DSU's dean of education and director of teacher apprenticeships (DSU; SD Department of Education).

House Bill 1232 (2025), Schwans' booth-sharing bill, changed the rules for salons and booths. It was signed by the governor on March 31, 2025 (SD Legislature).

Brighter Transitions Youth Treatment Center and Falls Academy share a licensed group care campus near Sioux Falls for youth ages 13 to 17 (SD Department of Social Services license).

The Encompassing Youth Reform Conference was held in Sioux Falls on July 7, 2025, hosted by Reps. Schwans, Bethany Soye, Heather Baxter and Novstrup and Sen. Joy Hohn. According to Schwans' write-up for the South Dakota Republican Party (July 13, 2025), 33 legislators, seven superintendents and nine youth facilities took part, and the superintendents spoke about the impact of SB 73.

SB 70, the Public Safety Improvement Act, passed in 2013 and moved many nonviolent offenders from prison to probation, drug courts and treatment. SB 73, the Juvenile Justice Public Safety Improvement Act, applied a similar approach to juveniles in 2015. Schwans recalls them as about five years old; they are older than that (SB 73 executive summary, SD Boards and Commissions).

The federal scholarship tax credit comes from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Under it, a taxpayer can give up to $1,700 a year ($3,400 for a joint return) to an approved scholarship-granting organization and take a dollar-for-dollar, nonrefundable federal tax credit, starting Jan. 1, 2027, in states that opt in. Gov. Rhoden opted South Dakota in on Nov. 14, 2025 (Office of the Governor; IRS).

Gov. Rhoden created the 30-member Correctional Rehabilitation Task Force by executive order after the Sept. 23, 2025, special session approved the new men's prison in Sioux Falls. Schwans and Sen. Hohn are members, and Lt. Gov. Tony Venhuizen chairs it. Its first meeting was Oct. 29, 2025, in Sioux Falls (Unified Judicial System; Dakota News Now).

Update (November 2026)

Cosmetology reciprocity: House Bill 1194, with Schwans as prime sponsor, lets the Cosmetology Commission credit work experience toward the education hours required of some out-of-state applicants, the idea she describes in this interview. It passed the House 64-1 and the Senate 30-3 and was signed by Gov. Rhoden on March 30, 2026 (SD Legislature).

Schwans' other 2026 cosmetology bills did not pass. House Bill 1165, to create basic and advanced esthetics licenses and revise the cosmetology licensing chapter, failed in the House 27-38 on Feb. 11, 2026. House Bill 1063, on natural hair braiding, was sent to the 41st day (killed) by House Commerce and Energy on Jan. 21, 2026 (SD Legislature).

Juvenile facilities: Senate Bill 220, with Sen. Hohn and Schwans as prime sponsors, would have paid for a full study of the state's juvenile correctional and residential facilities. It passed the Senate 24-9 but failed in the House 30-37 on March 4, 2026 (SD Legislature).

Charter schools: Senate Bill 218, with Schwans as the House prime sponsor, failed in the Senate on a 17-17 vote on Feb. 19, 2026 (SD Legislature).

The scholarship tax credit: South Dakota is on the IRS list of states taking part in the federal scholarship tax credit for 2027, so the credit is available to South Dakota donors starting Jan. 1, 2027 (IRS).

The Correctional Rehabilitation Task Force adopted 34 recommendations on Oct. 29, 2026, including starting rehabilitation programs when an inmate arrives rather than near release, more literacy, GED and technical school programs, and about $1.92 million a year for staff pay, peer support for people leaving prison, a landlord fund and transportation help. Its final report is due at its Nov. 30 meeting and will go to the 2027 Legislature (South Dakota Searchlight; SDPB).

The Teacher Apprenticeship Pathway is taking a fifth group of apprentices. Applications close Nov. 10, 2026, and classes begin in January 2027 (SD Department of Education).
