Should Local Government Be Involved in Non-Profit Fundraising?

Show notes

Published July 27, 2025, five days after Dan Goeller spoke during public comment at the July 22, 2025, Minnehaha County Commission meeting about the county running the Sioux Empire United Way's payroll-deduction campaign for its employees.

Guest

Dan Goeller of Sioux Falls, creator and founder of Leninists on the Prairie (YouTube and Substack), which covers DEI and woke issues in South Dakota. He was on Making Waves twice in 2023 as founder and executive director of Harmony South Dakota.

Background

July 22, 2025: during public comment, Goeller told the Minnehaha County Commission that having county payroll staff offer employees a United Way paycheck deduction showed "favoritism" to one charity. He objected to diversity, equity and inclusion measures in the United Way's funding applications and said just over $12,000 had gone to The Transformation Project. County Auditor Leah Anderson said about 103 of the county's 620 full-time employees (17 percent) give through payroll and that the paperwork costs the county money. Commissioner Cole Heisey objected to DEI standards and to preferential treatment of one charity. Commissioner Joe Kippley said he was "happy to support" the deduction and called critics "unserious people" looking for "a cause du jour." The commission took no action (Sioux Falls Live, July 22, 2025; The Dakota Scout, July 23, 2025; MitchellNow, July 24, 2025).

The DEI dispute goes back to 2023: Family Voice Action reported that Sioux Empire United Way had adopted an equity statement and asked funded agencies to report demographic data, and United Way leaders defended their DEI policy and said gender identity data is not a funding requirement (Family Voice Action, Dec. 7, 2023; The Dakota Scout, Nov. 20, 2023).

Some details Goeller gives in this interview come from documents he showed on camera and are his own account: the 2020 equity statement wording, the pronoun section on a child care funding form, the Augustana University professor at a DEI training for funded agencies, and a $12,445 community impact grant he says appears on the United Way's 2023 Form 990.

Update (November 2026)

Aug. 5, 2025: without a formal vote, the commission kept the United Way payroll option. Chairman Dean Karsky and Commissioner Gerald Beninga spoke in favor of keeping it, joining Kippley for a majority of the five-member board. Karsky said the campaign funds about 38 agencies and 74 programs, raised $9.2 million the year before with 99 percent kept local, and costs the county "only a few hours of time each year" (Sioux Falls Live, Aug. 7, 2025).

Aug. 11, 2025: Minnehaha County moved to eliminate affirmative action and its diversity committee to comply with a presidential executive order banning DEI in federal contracting (The Dakota Scout).

March 4, 2026: Sioux Empire United Way said its 2026 campaign raised $9,302,662, above its $9,264,349 goal, for 73 programs in 38 organizations (SiouxFalls.Business).

Sept. 2, 2026: the United Way launched its 2027 campaign with a $9,408,010 goal and a $1 million Sanford Health match for new and increased gifts through March 1, 2027. Its website showed $2,483,273 raised (26 percent) as of Oct. 20, 2026 (Dakota News Now; Sioux Empire United Way).

We found no report that Minnehaha County has dropped the payroll option since August 2025.
