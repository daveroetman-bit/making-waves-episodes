Show notes

Show notes

Published Aug. 16, 2025. Recorded in mid-August 2025 at a coffee shop on South Minnesota Avenue in Sioux Falls, about five weeks before the Sept. 23, 2025, special session on the new men's prison.

Guest

Jack Kolbeck, Republican of Sioux Falls, represents District 13 (Lincoln and Minnehaha counties) in the South Dakota House and sits on the Joint Committee on Appropriations. He served in the state Senate from 2017 to 2024, when he was term-limited. Gov. Larry Rhoden appointed him to the House on Feb. 5, 2025, to fill the seat Tony Venhuizen left to become lieutenant governor (SDPB, Feb. 5, 2025; South Dakota Searchlight, Feb. 5, 2025).

Background

The budget: the state closed fiscal year 2025 (ended June 30, 2025) with a $63 million surplus, but sales tax revenue finished $3.7 million below estimates; the gap was covered by more than $47 million in unexpected unclaimed property receipts (SDPB, July 14, 2025). On July 25, 2025, lawmakers were told the state could face a $24.5 million revenue shortfall in fiscal 2026 (South Dakota Searchlight). The "$67 million" figure in the interview is Kolbeck's own number from memory.

The prison plan Kolbeck describes ($650 million, 1,500 beds with 1,200 secure and 300 minimum-security, about 147 acres in northeast Sioux Falls, the Lincoln County land traded in the deal, the Hill holding about 735 men in space built for 450) is his own account as of August 2025, before the special session.

The vaping tax idea (extending South Dakota's nicotine tax to vaping products and putting the money into a fund for victims' services; he names VOCA, the federal Victims of Crime Act fund) and his figure that 38 states tax vaping are his own account. So are the Aeronautics Fund details ($150,000 a year per airport, 57 airports) and the homeschool scholarship ACT scores.

Update (November 2026)

Sept. 23, 2025: in a one-day special session the Legislature passed Senate Bill 2, the $650 million, 1,500-bed men's prison in northeast Sioux Falls, 24-11 in the Senate (exactly two-thirds) and 51-18 in the House. Gov. Rhoden signed it that evening (South Dakota Searchlight; SD Legislature bill status).

April 22, 2026: state leaders broke ground on the new men's prison at 3400 N. Bahnson Ave. The lead contractor, JE Dunn, predicted it would come in under its $650 million guaranteed maximum price; completion is expected in 2029 (South Dakota Searchlight; Sioux Falls Live/The Dakota Scout).

July 10, 2026: the ribbon was cut on the new women's prison in Rapid City. The first inmates moved in that September, and it held 79 women at the end of the month, while part of the old Pierre campus stays in use (The Rapid City Post, July 10 and Oct. 4, 2026). That was earlier than the December 2026 opening Kolbeck expected. The complex cost $87 million, not the roughly $80 million he cited.

2026 session: Kolbeck stayed on Appropriations and voted for the $7.5 billion budget (HB 1326, passed 50-17 on March 12, 2026) and for HB 1200, an appropriation for victim services provided by nonprofit organizations (Pierre Review voting record; The Dakota Scout, March 12, 2026). No vaping tax passed. Lawmakers instead passed HB 1220 and SB 221, which regulate and license nicotine product sales (SD Legislature).

Airports: the Legislature approved, and Rhoden signed in March 2026, a law allowing loans of up to $15 million each at 2% to the Sioux Falls and Rapid City airports for terminal expansions; the Rules Review Committee cleared the rules on July 14, 2026 (South Dakota Searchlight).

Election: Kolbeck ran for the House again. He finished first in the June 2, 2026, District 13 Republican primary (2,237 votes, 35.3%), ahead of Rep. John Hughes (2,118) and Nick Kotzea (1,991). He and Hughes face Democrat Ali Horsted for the district's two House seats in the Nov. 3, 2026, general election (Ballotpedia, certified results). Results were not in when this was posted.
