Show notes

Published May 31, 2025, three months after the Legislature voted down the $825 million Lincoln County prison plan and while Gov. Larry Rhoden's Project Prison Reset task force was searching for a new plan. This is part 1 of a series; part 2 (July 26, 2025) covers solutions for overcrowding and recidivism.

Guest

Sen. Kevin Jensen, R-Canton, South Dakota Senate District 16 (in the Senate since January 2025; previously in the House); six years on the state Corrections Commission; by his account in this interview, also six years on the Juvenile Justice Reform Oversight Council and a Minnehaha County Jail officer in the 1980s

Background

The Lincoln County plan: the state put $52 million into a 1,500-bed men's prison in rural southern Lincoln County, and lawmakers rejected the $825 million plan in February 2025. When the project later moved to Sioux Falls, the governor's office said about $21 million of the Lincoln County spending could not be recovered (South Dakota Searchlight).

Metal Craft Industries: the private company's shop inside the Jameson Annex employed maximum-security inmates at market wages for more than two decades. It moved out in early 2024 after the Department of Corrections required it to switch to work-release inmates. The DOC said the company chose to leave. Between 2020 and 2024 its inmate workers earned $3.6 million in wages and paid more than $1.3 million for room and board (DOC figures reported by South Dakota Searchlight, Sept. 27, 2024).

The Jameson Annex (now Jameson Prison) opened in February 1993 next to the State Penitentiary and is the state's Level V, maximum-security facility (Department of Corrections).

Figures Jensen gives in this interview are his own: about 30 Metal Craft inmate employees paid $17 to $22 an hour; 540 original maximum-security beds at the Jameson plus the 192-bed D-Pod (opened in 2006 and, he says, built to take a second floor); 142 maximum-security inmates at the end of 2024 and 192 at the end of April 2025; about 200 Jameson inmates eligible for work release but only nine out; and more than $30 million put into the penitentiary building over 13 years.

Update (November 2026)

Sept. 23, 2025: in a special session, the Legislature approved a 1,500-bed, $650 million men's prison in northeast Sioux Falls, about 3 miles from the penitentiary. Jensen voted no, saying a new building won't fix the policies and the lack of programming inside the prisons. Corrections Secretary Kellie Wasko had announced her resignation earlier that month (SDPB, South Dakota Searchlight, Sioux Falls Live).

April 22, 2026: the state broke ground on the new prison. The contractor said it is on track to come in under its $650 million guaranteed maximum price, with completion expected in 2029 (South Dakota Searchlight). The Department of Corrections lists 2030.

Aug. 5, 2026: the penitentiary lost its fourth warden in five years in a senior staff shake-up under Corrections Secretary Nick Lamb (South Dakota Searchlight).

Oct. 4, 2026: the new $87 million, 288-bed women's prison in Rapid City began taking inmates in September and held 79 women at the end of the month (South Dakota Searchlight).

Oct. 29, 2026: consultants told Gov. Rhoden's Correctional Rehabilitation Task Force that it will cost at least $1.9 million a year to start fixing rehabilitation in the prison system. A Rhoden policy adviser estimated the new men's prison will cost $44.6 million a year to run, about $20 million more than current Sioux Falls prison operations. Jensen had warned in September 2025 of about $30 million in added operating costs (his estimate). The task force's final report is due Nov. 30 (South Dakota Searchlight, Hub City Radio/KELO).

Jensen is unopposed for re-election to Senate District 16 on Nov. 3, 2026 (Ballotpedia).
