Transparency is for Government

Show notes

Published March 10, 2025. Austin Goss's last day at The Dakota Scout was Thursday, March 6. Early on Friday, March 7, Fox News Digital ran a story built on a cease-and-desist letter from former Gov. Kristi Noem's personal lawyer, and that is the "hit piece" discussed here.

Guest

Austin Goss, investigative and political reporter for The Dakota Scout until March 6, 2025, and before that capitol bureau chief for Gray Television (Dakota News Now), based in Pierre

Background

The Scout requested the state credit card records in May 2024. State Auditor Rich Sattgast denied the request, citing security and a state law that kept a governor's records closed for 10 years. The Scout sued in September 2024, the case was settled after Noem left office, and thousands of pages of redacted records were turned over on Feb. 6, 2025. Gov. Larry Rhoden's office first demanded the records back, then dropped the threat.

The records show more than $650,000 charged on state-issued cards used by Noem and her office from January 2019 through April 2024, mostly travel, lodging and food. The Scout and Sioux Falls Live published a searchable database of more than 1,200 of the charges.

The March 6, 2025 letter from Noem's lawyer, Trevor Stanley, said Noem personally used her card 30 times for a total of $2,056.72, demanded corrections, and threatened a lawsuit seeking damages "that we estimate at millions of dollars." The Scout stood by its reporting.

Attorney General Marty Jackley released his own travel expense vouchers on March 4, 2025: 37 vouchers totaling about $7,242.54. He said he doesn't have a state-issued credit card.

Lawmakers had already shortened the wait on a governor's closed records. SB 10 (2025) cut it from 10 years to five years after the governor leaves office (Senate 34-0, House 59-7, signed Feb. 11, 2025).

Update (October 2026)

We found no report that Noem's lawyer followed through with a lawsuit against The Scout. A week after the Fox story, Sioux Falls Live reported that a political action committee tied to Noem, the American Resolve Policy Fund, was running paid posts sharing it. The Scout marked its 200th print edition in July 2026 and still points to the credit card case as one of its fights for open records.

Noem stayed at Homeland Security until March 2026, when President Trump removed her and named then-Sen. Markwayne Mullin to replace her. She then served six months as special envoy to the Shield of the Americas, and CNN reported on Oct. 16, 2026 that she is now a senior adviser at the State Department.

Goss wrote in an Aug. 31, 2025 Aberdeen Insider column that he and his family were moving back east to be closer to family after he spent most of 2025 on active-duty military orders. In 2026 he was spokesman for Jon Hansen's campaign for governor. After Hansen lost the June primary, Goss backed Toby Doeden in the July 28 runoff, which Gov. Larry Rhoden won 83,590 to 34,435.
